Home &raquo; Progressive Dairy &raquo; U.S. dairy herd growth drives July cow culling higher Dairy Industry News U.S. dairy herd growth drives July cow culling higher Audrey Schmitz August 24, 2026 With the U.S. dairy herd continuing to expand, it follows that more cows are available for normal herd turnover, resulting in higher culling totals than a year ago. June dairy cow slaughter increased from both the previous month and the same month a year ago, reflecting the larger overall herd. Even with more cows marketed for slaughter, the pace of culling remains generally in line with herd expansion. Email Audrey Schmitz Related U.S. dairy herd growth drives higher milk production in May Larger U.S. dairy herd drives higher June cow culling U.S. dairy herd again on growth track Based on the latest USDA monthly Livestock Slaughter data released on Aug. 20, the number of dairy cull cows marketed through U.S. slaughter plants in July 2026 was estimated at 227,600. While up 19,500 from June, it was also 1,800 more than July 2025. July 2025 had 26 non-holiday weekdays and Saturdays while July 2026 also had 26 days. Slaughter averaged 8,800 head per business day this year, up 100 head from a year earlier. Weekly dairy cow slaughter toward the end of 2025 reversed a long-term trend in which weekly slaughter had trailed year-earlier levels, with a total decline of nearly 556,100 head. Since September 2025, slaughter has increased 95,200 head compared with the same period a year earlier, despite declines in two of the past four weeks. The USDA estimated the July 2026 U.S. dairy herd at 9.71 million dairy cows, up 199,000 cows from July 2025. It was also unchanged from the June estimate and put the July culling rate at about 2.3% of the herd. Based on the monthly data, year-to-date (January-July) dairy cull cow slaughter now stands at about 1,565,800 head, up 64,600 from the same period a year ago. That was followed in the Northeast (Arizona, California, Hawaii and Nevada) at 40,900 head. Other monthly regional totals were estimated at 33,600 head in Delaware, Maryland, Pennsylvania, West Virginia and Virginia; 30,900 head in Arkansas, Louisiana, New Mexico, Oklahoma and Texas; and 30,700 head in Alaska, Idaho, Oregon and Washington. Primary data for the USDA’s Livestock Slaughter   report is obtained from reports from about 1,100 federally inspected plants and nearly 1,825 state-inspected or custom-exempt slaughter plants.
