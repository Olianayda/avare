What began as an environmental concern has evolved into a commercial self-feeding system: feed additives claiming to cut emissions, precision devices designed to measure gas from the cow's’ muzzle and entire research programs funded to chase incremental reductions. There is now an entire industry built around methane from cows. Even new academic positions and carbon innovation projects have emerged to support the effort. While the purpose is to achieve a net zero dairy, where milk production leaves no greenhouse gas (GHG) footprint, the collateral effect is another.
