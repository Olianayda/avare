USDA ends mandatory farmer funding of ESG commitments in dairy checkoff U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke L. Rollins announced the USDA’s decisive action to end dairy checkoff funding for environmental, social and governance (ESG) overregulation. “American dairy producers, cattle ranchers and farmers pay checkoff assessments so those dollars can build demand for their products – not bankroll radical climate agendas that raise costs and constrain production,” Rollins said. “Today’s action returns the dairy checkoff and all research and promotion programs to their core mission: expanding markets and supporting the hardworking men and women who feed this country. We will not allow producer dollars to underwrite mandates that put American agriculture at a disadvantage.” Under the Dairy Production Stabilization Act, checkoff funds are authorized for promotion, research and nutrition education that strengthen markets for U.S. dairy. The Innovation Center for U.S. Dairy, established through the checkoff, has pursued ESG initiatives, including greenhouse gas and net zero targets. The USDA’s action ends checkoff support for those ESG-related projects while allowing necessary administrative functions that do not advance such agendas. Advertisement This action also directs the Agricultural Marketing Service to ensure no research and promotion funds in other commodity checkoffs advance ESG mandates. First interest rate hike in three years The Federal Reserve raised interest rates in a 12-0 vote at the organization’s Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting Sept. 15-16. The hike of a quarter percent to between 3.75% and 4%, effectively immediately, is the first rise in interest rates since 2023. Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh said the unanimous vote to raise interest rates is the unfavorable results of sustained elevated inflation. “The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long,” said Warsh in a press conference Sept. 16. “The committee’s unanimous vote shows our resolve to achieve price stability on a timelier basis.” While the committee noted economic activity has expanded solidly, productivity growth is strong and capital investment is robust, inflation remains higher than the committee’s 2% goal. Warsh eluded in the press conference that tensions in the Middle East were also a factor in the announcement. As a result, the 12 committee members’ decision is an effort to deliver price stability to the American people. At the September meeting, the committee released updated projections, which indicate another interest rate may be possible yet this year. The next FOMC meeting is set for Oct. 27-28. Fluid milk sales up again in July With nearly 3.4 billion pounds of packaged fluid milk products shipped by milk processors in July 2026, fluid milk sales were up 0.2% from July 2025. According to data from the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service:
Total sales: July 2026 sales of packaged fluid milk products were estimated at 3.4 billion pounds, up 0.2% from the same month a year earlier. At 24.5 billion pounds, year-to-date sales of all fluid products are 0.1% higher than last year.
Conventional products: Monthly sales totaled 3.1 billion pounds, up 0.5% from the same month a year earlier. Sales of flavored whole milk were 23.5% higher than last year. Year-to-date 2026 sales were estimated at 22.7 billion pounds, which is up 0.2% from last year.
Organic products: July sales totaled 235 million pounds, down 3% than a year earlier. Sales were up 16.1% for flavored whole milk. Year-to-date organic fluid milk sales were estimated at 1.7 billion pounds, down 0.6% from this time last year. Organic represented about 7% of total fluid product sales in July.
The U.S. figures are based on consumption of fluid milk products in FMMO areas, which account for approximately 92% of total U.S. fluid milk sales, and adding the other 8% from outside FMMO-regulated areas. Sales outlets include food stores, convenience stores, warehouse stores/wholesale clubs, nonfood stores, schools, the food service industry and home delivery. GDT index down 1.1% After four higher trading events, the price index of dairy product prices sold on the Global Dairy Trade (GDT) platform was down 1.1% in the auction held Sept. 15 . Compared to the previous auction, prices for individual product categories were mostly lower. Cheddar cheese was up a whopping 16.5%, and skim milk powder was up 0.1%. The remaining products were lower. Mozzarella and butter were down 6% and 5.7%, respectively. Lactose was down 4.3%, and anhydrous milkfat was down 3%. Whole milk powder and buttermilk powder were down by less than 1%. The GDT platform offers dairy products from several global companies: Fonterra (New Zealand), Darigold, Valley Milk and Dairy America (U.S.), Inalpi (Italy), Arla (Denmark), Arla Foods Ingredients (Denmark), BMI (Germany), Kerry Dairy (Ireland) and Solarec (Belgium). The next GDT auction is Oct. 6. National Dairy Board awards scholarships to 12 college students The National Dairy Promotion and Research Board (NDB), through Dairy Management Inc. – which manages the national dairy checkoff – annually awards $2,500 scholarships to 11 students. In addition, the NDB awards a $3,500 James H. Loper Jr. Memorial Scholarship to one outstanding scholarship recipient. Eligible majors include journalism, communications/public relations, marketing, business, economics, nutrition, food science and agricultural education. Criteria include academic achievement, career interest in a dairy-related discipline and demonstrated leadership, initiative and integrity. Sienna Mahaffy, a student at Oklahoma State University majoring in business administration, has been named the 2026-27 James H. Loper Jr. Memorial Scholarship recipient. Students earning $2,500 scholarships are: Trell Amoss, Oklahoma State University; Cassie Bohnert, Iowa State University; Campbell Booth, University of Minnesota; Eliza Dugan, West Texas A&M University; Sara Kennedy, Pennsylvania State University; Keegan Medeiros, California State University – Monterey Bay; Nicholas Needham, University of Wisconsin – Madison; Ella Raatz, University of Minnesota; Elle Ruffner, Iowa State University; Taylor Thigpen, University of Notre Dame; and Kelsey Timp, Iowa State University. MilkPEP launches Milkscape to revolutionize category intelligence for dairy milk The Milk Processor Education Program (MilkPEP) announced the launch of Milkscape, a comprehensive data and insights platform designed for the dairy milk category. Serving as a centralized center of excellence, Milkscape provides participating processors and retailers of all sizes with a single, connected source for cutting-edge category intelligence, allowing them to spot market opportunities and drive strategic growth. Major consumer packaged goods and beverage brands routinely run sophisticated dashboards and intelligence platforms to guide their decisions, and a category as highly significant as dairy milk deserves the same caliber of tools. Milkscape closes this gap, translating complex research into actionable, retail-friendly strategies. The urgency for this platform is highlighted by the category’s unmatched volume at retail, where 92% of U.S. households purchase milk annually, and 63% of Americans use it on an average day, according to Circana. “Nearly every household in America purchases milk – the category deserves best-in-class intelligence,” said Al Dejewski, MilkPEP’s senior vice president of marketing. “Milkscape gives processors and retailers the modern insights they need to make smarter decisions and identify tomorrow’s opportunities. By unifying our research into one fast, connected ecosystem, we are helping our partners anticipate market shifts with total confidence.” Milkscape unifies MilkPEP’s category leadership resources, including monthly newsletters, the website, insights materials and media coverage, into a single, cohesive ecosystem. A new dynamic weekly dashboard will serve as a supplement to the monthly Sales Topline report, delivering a weekly refresh on topline trends across dairy milk and its segments so processors can act on trends faster. The platform directly addresses the major issues facing the industry today, such as volume shifts between dairy and plant-based alternatives, the macro trends driving lactose-free and organic growth, small format challenges and the evolving habits of today’s Generation Alpha shoppers. Underlying these inquiries is a fundamental retail truth: Milk is an indispensable basket builder. Recent Circana Panel data reveals that grocery baskets containing dairy milk ring up nearly twice as high per trip ($88.79) as those without it ($46.23), proving that maximizing milk placement inherently captures broader store growth.
