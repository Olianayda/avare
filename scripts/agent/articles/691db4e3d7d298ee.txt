Dairy farming TV series premieres at World Dairy Expo On Sept. 29 at the World Dairy Expo, the team behind the grain entrapment film SILO will be screening the premiere episode of their newest fictional project about agriculture, the six-part limited TV series Perfect Sundays . The show’s producer and creator, Sam Goldberg, will be joined by movie and TV star Jim Parrack for a presentation about the series and its innovative approach to using fictional storytelling as an emotional outlet for American farmers. Perfect Sundays captures the journey of a family that digs its way out of economic ruin and emotional paralysis. The story is familiar to farmers, no doubt, but also to anyone who recognizes the internal battles of depression and its devastating effects on a family. Set in the heart of rural America, this series makes universal the story of one farm family who knows they must keep trying despite it all. Now, they just have to agree on how. To watch Perfect Sundays , fans can engage in their local communities to host events in whatever mode available to them – from churches and high school auditoriums – to local movie theaters and other cultural institutions. And until the end of the year, the show is also available to stream directly from the   Perfect Sundays website for those who cannot make it to a local, in-person event. Applications open for Holstein Foundation’s inaugural Dairy Data Academy Applications are now open for the Holstein Foundation’s inaugural Dairy Data Academy, a four-day immersive program designed to introduce a select group of young people to the data, technologies and dairy science shaping the future of the dairy industry. Advertisement Selected participants will receive a largely all-expenses-paid experience, with travel, lodging, meals and program activities covered by the Holstein Foundation. Held Aug. 2-5, 2027, in Bowling Green, Kentucky, Dairy Data Academy combines hands-on experiences at the Western Kentucky University (WKU) SmartHolstein Lab with interactive classroom learning. Participants will explore how information, emerging technologies and innovation are being used to support decision-making on today’s dairy farms, while building practical skills through collaborative and experiential learning. Applications are open to U.S. citizens and residents, no younger than 16 and no older than 21 years old, as of Aug. 2, 2027, and who meet one of the following criteria:
Be a high school junior or senior as of the fall 2027 academic term
Be enrolled in an accredited two-year, four-year or vocational postsecondary program as of the fall 2027 academic term
Hold a high school diploma (or equivalent) and be currently employed on a dairy farm
Students and young professionals interested in agriculture, technology, data science, analytics, computer science or related disciplines are encouraged to apply. No prior dairy or agricultural experience is required. Applications will be accepted through Dec. 1. Approximately 30 participants will be selected for the inaugural Dairy Data Academy cohort based on their demonstrated interest in the program, communication skills, initiative, curiosity and potential to contribute to a collaborative learning environment. Upon notification of acceptance on or before Feb. 1 participants will be required to submit a nonrefundable $100 registration deposit within 30 days to confirm their participation. To learn more about the academy or apply, visit the  Dairy Data Academy website . National DHIA offers veterinary student scholarships If you’re a third- or fourth-year college of veterinary medicine student, apply for the National Dairy Herd Information Association (DHIA) veterinary medicine scholarships. Applicants must be enrolled at a college that is accredited by the American Veterinary Medical Association Council on Education. Successful applicants will receive a $1,500 scholarship. To apply for a National DHIA Veterinary Student Scholarship, applicants must submit a letter of application and two reference letters. The letter of application requires students to submit information regarding their area of interest/specialty, academic background, experience using DHI data, career plans, issues challenging dairy veterinary medicine and solutions to address those challenges. For more information, contact Elizabeth “Liz” Straw , National DHIA scholarship chair. Specific application requirements are posted on the National DHIA website . Send completed applications by Dec. 31. Recipients will be announced at the National DHIA Leadership Sessions and 62nd Annual Business Meeting, being held March 2-4, Fort Worth, Texas. National DHIA Scholarship Committee members will evaluate applicants based on overall interest as a veterinarian planning to work in dairy, involvement in dairy medicine and extracurricular activities, and interest in using dairy software and dairy records to aid in dairy management and in improving animal health. IDFA showcases high-quality dairy protein at All-American Meat Up The International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) sponsored the All-American Meat Up at Nationals Park, joining partners from across the animal protein community to highlight for members of Congress, administration officials and their staffs the important role high-quality protein foods – including dairy – play in healthy diets. IDFA members Sargento and Blue Bell Creameries donated products for the event, giving policymakers an opportunity to experience firsthand the taste, versatility and protein dairy foods can deliver. Sargento provided cheese curds fried in beef tallow, cheese slices for sliders and shredded cheese for hot dog and chili dog toppings. Blue Bell Creameries provided PRO pints, featuring 33 grams or more of protein per pint, for caterers to scoop and serve to guests. “It is important that policymakers understand the unique nutrition dairy brings to the American diet, and protein is an important part of that story,” said Michael Dykes, president and CEO of IDFA. “The protein in milk, cheese and yogurt is high quality and complete, meaning it naturally provides all nine essential amino acids our bodies need but cannot make on their own. That makes dairy an easy, nutritious way for Americans to get the protein they need from foods they know, love and enjoy every day. We were proud to join our partners across the animal protein community to bring that message directly to policymakers and to showcase the innovation happening across dairy.” Held as part of the Meat Institute’s inaugural Meat Week, the All-American Meat Up brought together leaders from across the food value chain to demonstrate industry unity and celebrate American agriculture. Outstanding individuals honored at All-American Dairy Show Distinguished individuals were recognized for contributions that have shaped the All-American Dairy Show. Dale Olver of State College, Pennsylvania, received the Obie Snider Award, and Tom and Joy Crothers of Quarryville, Pennsylvania, received the Image Award. Reid and Diane Hoover of Annville, Pennsylvania, were selected as the 2026 recipients of the Dairy Hall of Fame Award. The Obie Snider Award, established in memory of All-American Dairy Show founding father Obie Snider, recognizes an individual who values service to the dairy industry and community and displays high standards of conduct. This year’s recipient, Dale Olver, has made a lifelong commitment to dairy education, youth development and the dairy industry, leaving a lasting impact on generations of students and dairy youth. After earning a degree in dairy production from Penn State, Olver spent 10 years with Atlantic Breeders Cooperative as a progeny analyst under the guidance of Harry Roth. In 1989, Olver began his career at Penn State, where he has educated and mentored students for more than three decades. He has coached the Penn State Dairy Judging Team and co-advises the Penn State Dairy Science Club with Chad Dechow. Under Olver’s leadership, the dairy judging team has captured three national championships and earned more than 30 top 10 finishes at the national contest, helping Penn State rank second among universities in all-time national contest winning percentage. His commitment to youth development also reaches throughout Pennsylvania and beyond. In addition to his responsibilities at Penn State, Olver works closely with the Pennsylvania 4-H dairy program and supports events such as the Pennsylvania Junior Dairy Show and the Junior Dairy Management Contest at the All-American Dairy Show. He also helps coordinate dairy quiz bowl and dairy jeopardy competitions for Pennsylvania’s Holstein, Guernsey and Jersey associations. The Image Award is presented annually to an individual who has enhanced the image of the All-American Dairy Show through notable contributions to its reputation, prestige and welfare. The 2026 recipients, Tom and Joy Crothers, are longtime supporters whose service has touched nearly every part of the All-American Dairy Show. Over the years, they have volunteered with judging and showmanship contests, the ultrasound booth, Guernsey and Brown Swiss check-in, Guernsey class organization, housekeeping awards and youth events. For the past four years, the Crotherses have served on the Hospitality Committee. Each morning, they help prepare and distribute coffee, doughnuts and milk throughout the barns, offering exhibitors a warm welcome as the show comes to life. Joy has helped secure funding for milk through Allied Milk while coordinating supplies, monitoring inventory and ensuring equitable distribution. The Dairy Hall of Fame award recognizes the service and accomplishments of Pennsylvania dairy leaders based on their character, leadership and commitment to youth development within the dairy industry. For 44 years, the Hoovers have operated Brook-Corner Holsteins, a registered Holstein herd with 375 milking cows and about 400 acres. They now farm in partnership with their son Brad and his wife, Amy. The Hoover family includes four children and nine grandchildren. The family has exhibited cattle at local, state and national shows, earning All-Pennsylvania and All-American nominations and awards. The Hoovers also have hosted farm tours for students from preschool through college, including dairy judging teams, and hosted the Northeast Intercollegiate Dairy Challenge. He also has served on the boards of the Lebanon County and Pennsylvania Holstein associations, including as president, and has represented Pennsylvania numerous times as a delegate to the Holstein Association USA national convention. His additional leadership roles include service on the boards of the Center for Dairy Excellence, Pennsylvania Dairymen’s Association and Center for Dairy Excellence Foundation. He has chaired each organization and serves on the advisory committee for the Little Dutchmen FFA chapter. Diane has been a member of the Lebanon County Dairy Promotion Committee for 35 years and is a longtime leader in the Lebanon County 4-H dairy club. She served as a dairy representative and chair of the Pennsylvania Beef Council and recently completed a six-year term representing Pennsylvania on the Cattlemen’s Beef Board, which oversees funds collected through the Beef Checkoff program. Founder Kate Colehour earned $20,000 in prize funding to help scale dairy-based innovation for her growing brand. The competition marked the finale of the Midwest Dairy Accelerator, an intensive 10-week virtual program designed by Midwest Dairy in partnership with innovation advisory firm VentureFuel, to help emerging food and beverage brands scale innovative products that use Midwest dairy as a key ingredient. Throughout the program, founders gained access to workshops, one-on-one mentorship and direct collaboration with leaders across the dairy ecosystem, including ingredient experts, processors, manufacturers, retailers, academics and food industry executives.
