Beef-on-dairy: Having a plan before calves hit the ground Jeffrey Lutz September 2, 2026 Having a buyer relationship established before beef-on-dairy calves are born can help maintain a revenue stream when the market inevitably swings. The beef-on-dairy calf markets are at or near all-time highs. For dairy producers who have made the shift to beef semen, the revenue from these beef-on-dairy calves has become a meaningful source of income, and it is something that can easily be taken for granted when the market is cooperating. Feeder demands shift. And the dairies that will continue capturing top value even when conditions change aren’t the ones reacting to the market; they’re the ones who planned ahead of it. That planning starts with a single question: When your next calf hits the ground, do you already know exactly who wants it and why? Every calf starts with a breeding decision Every beef-on-dairy calf is the result of a breeding decision made months earlier. That decision – which bull, which semen, which traits – determines not just what that calf is worth today, but whether it is the kind of calf the market will continue asking for. There is nothing wrong with producing a commodity beef-on-dairy calf. The market will take it. But there is a big difference between producing a calf the market will accept and producing one that buyers are actively seeking out. Advertisement Feeders and packers are becoming increasingly selective about what they want. Feed conversion, red meat yield and dressing percentage are crucial to a feeding operation. They are the difference between a profitable pen and a break-even one. From the dairy’s view, we know sire selection needs to align with those downstream goals – growth and carcass traits – while also prioritizing calving ease to protect the cow. If both things can be achieved, the result is a calf with a different value. The right partner changes everything Choosing the right sires is only half the equation; the other half is knowing where those calves are going before they are born. That is where a committed, long-term program partner changes the game. The strongest programs are built around structured alignment between what the dairy needs and what the feeder wants. These programs are built before breeding decisions are made and maintained through the life of the calf. That means working with a buyer who isn’t just willing to take calves when the market is hot, but who has committed to taking consistent, predictable volume because they helped define what that calf looks like from the start. On the flip side for the dairy, this kind of partnership equates to predictability. You know your calves have a home. You know the genetics used will meet the buyer’s standards. You won’t be scrambling when the spot market softens or buyer preferences shift. Well-built programs also prioritize selecting for fertility and calving ease because if a cow doesn’t settle or has trouble calving, the end results don’t help anyone. For the feeder, confidence comes from consistency. When calves come from genetics with proven fertility, more calves are born per breeding event which creates predictable and plannable volume. With a focus on sire selection built around feed conversion and red meat yield, the feeder isn’t guessing about pen performance. When dairy goals and feeder goals are genuinely aligned, each side is getting what matters most to them. The program works for everyone. The real cost of no plan When calf markets are strong, it is easy to assume you are doing fine. The check clears, the calves move, and the question about going on a program gets pushed to next quarter. But strong markets have a way of masking misalignment that shows up clearly when conditions tighten. The hidden conversation most producers are having isn’t about premiums. It is about discounts they are absorbing without fully accounting for them. Calves without traceability. Calves from bulls with unknown carcass data. Calves with inconsistent birthweights or health histories. Each of those variables is a reason for a buyer to bid less or pass entirely. In a hot market, those discounts feel manageable. In a softer market, they define your margin. The producers who feel it first when the market shifts are the ones without a plan. Once a market shift happens, you are left selling into a spot market that no longer needs to compete for calves. The producers who weather market changes the best are the ones whose calves were already spoken for – because the program they are in created demand that isn’t purely market-dependent. Full alignment – the right sires, a committed buyer and consistent calf quality – is also where premiums live. But the more immediate case for planning isn’t chasing upside. It’s protecting the value you’re already generating before the market reminds you it can move in both directions. What should a good beef-on-dairy program include? Not all beef-on-dairy programs are created equal. As more genetic companies and calf buyers have entered the space, the range of what’s being called a program has widened considerably. Before committing, dairy producers should be asking pointed questions about what a program actually delivers. Here are a few things every dairy producer should expect from a good beef-on-dairy program. A committed buyer This is the foundation. A program that can’t guarantee a home for your calves before they’re born isn’t a program; it is a preferred customer arrangement that evaporates when the market softens. Look for a structured, long-term buyer commitment that exists independent of spot market conditions. Sire fertility and calving ease standards Any program worth joining has a defined threshold for the bulls it uses. Fertility-proven sires with documented Calving Ease aren’t optional. They are the baseline. If a program can’t tell you exactly how its sires will perform in these areas, that is a red flag. Traceability Buyers who are making long-term commitments want to know what they are getting. Programs built around individual calf identification and data capture give both the dairy and the buyer a track record to build on and a story to tell as traceability requirements in the beef supply chain have and will continue to grow. Consistency at scale A program that works for one pen doesn’t necessarily work for a thousand calves. The right program can absorb consistent volume, maintain sire selection standards across that volume and deliver a uniform calf that buyers can plan around. Alignment, not just access The best programs aren’t simply connecting dairies to buyers. They are building a supply chain where every participant – the genetics provider, the dairy and the feeder – has skin in the same outcome. Plan now while you have the leverage The beef-on-dairy opportunity is real, and right now the market is rewarding producers who are in it. But opportunity without a plan is just luck, and luck is not a business strategy. The dairies that will continue to capture strong calf value five years from now aren’t necessarily the ones with the most cows or the biggest operations. They are the ones who treated their beef-on-dairy program like what it is: a revenue stream worth protecting. That means making genetic and sire selection decisions based on what the entire supply chain values, not just what is available. It means having a buyer relationship built before the calf was born, not after. And it means being in a program that was designed around alignment, where genetics, the commitment and the market demand are all pointed in the same direction. The market is good today. The question worth asking right now, while you have the leverage of a strong market and time on your side, is whether your program is built to hold its value when the market eventually shifts. If the answer is anything less than yes, now is exactly the right time to find one that is.
