Virtual victory: World Dairy Expo to celebrate 25 years of virtual farm tours Patryce Eldredge LeFevre September 9, 2026 Virtual farm tours have been a valued part of World Dairy Expo for 25 years, allowing dairy farmers to share their farm story, fostering education and connection with consumers and producers alike. In-person farm tours around the Madison, Wisconsin, area were once a popular attraction at World Dairy Expo. In 2001, amid the foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) outbreak in Europe, the world stage of the dairy industry made an historic change: The farm tours held during World Dairy Expo became a virtual experience. This year marks the 25th anniversary of that transition, celebrating the success and lasting impact of the virtual tours. Instead, we can highlight exceptional dairies across the country or around the globe,” says Jenna Langrehr, World Dairy Expo attendee programs manager. “It’s been kind of a challenge turned opportunity.” Langrehr says the virtual farm tours have remained a favorite among attendees. Advances in technology have also opened new avenues for showcasing farms, allowing producers from countries such as Sweden to participate and enabling tours to be presented through live video broadcasts. The key word: Opportunity Participating in the farm tours provides valuable experiences for all members of the industry. Advertisement “We saw the virtual farm tour as an incredible opportunity to share our passion for farming with people who may never have the chance to step onto a dairy farm,” says Laura Raatz, who presented the Wagner Farms tour last year. “It's easy to assume that people won't be interested in the details of dairy farming, but we found the opposite to be true. When people are invited to learn and ask questions in a welcoming environment, they're genuinely curious.” Raatz says the experience of presenting allowed her to connect with consumers, students, educators and other farmers, building trust by showing what happens on their farm every day. She says it opened the door for meaningful conversations, questions and feedback from people who wanted to learn more. Trinity Johnson from Johnson’s Rolling Acres, which was spotlighted in 2014, says that while many farmers may not be very outgoing or are hesitant to participate, it is important for farmers to take the opportunity to put themselves out there. “Do it. Don’t even think. Be proud of what you’ve accomplished and that you were asked to be a part of this,” Johnson says. “This is your opportunity to set your story straight. We’re not going to let somebody else tell our story. Keep our industry in the spotlight in a good way.” Giving credit where credit is due The virtual farm tours are not just an educational program. Raatz says that for Wagner Farms, a fifth-generation family farm, the tour was a chance to honor previous generations and highlight the hard work, sacrifices and values that have been passed down through the years. She says that while preparing for the tour, it was not so much about getting the farm ready but rather deciding what story they wanted to tell viewers for a genuine understanding of who they are, what they value and why they’re passionate about dairy farming. While Langrehr helps organize the virtual farm tours, she does not solely select the farms. Instead, the unique approach allows participating companies to nominate and sponsor farms they see as successful and would like to highlight. The sponsors then work with the farms to gather content for the tour. Langrehr says the process serves as a marketing opportunity for sponsors while also highlighting successful dairy operations. Efficient and effective education Langrehr says the virtual aspect of the farm tours makes them an efficient education tool and allows farms of all kinds from any location to be showcased. Farmers, consumers and students attending World Dairy Expo can see farms from coast to coast without ever leaving Madison. Even those who are unable to attend in person can watch the farm tours later. “Virtual tours make agriculture accessible. Not everyone has the opportunity to visit a farm, but nearly everyone can experience one through a screen,” Raatz says. “They allow viewers to see real farming practices, hear directly from farmers and gain a better understanding of how food is produced.” Johnson says he is surprised that his farm’s tour from 12 years ago is still available for people to find and learn from. He says it is neat to see the views on YouTube and the video’s reach. Though technology makes the tours an efficient education tool, the emotional, personable nature of the events also makes them an effective education tool. “I think you have to emotionally connect with people. Education is never going to be able to overpower emotion,” Johnson says. “I feel that people would rather know who Johnson’s Rolling Acres is and what we do, what we try to do for our community, for our employees, for our families and to see that we take care of our animals rather than knowing what particularly our farming practices are.” Raatz agrees. “I truly believe that people don’t care how much you know until they know how much you care,” she adds. “That authenticity creates meaningful conversations, builds trust and strengthens the connection between farmers and consumers in a way that facts and statistics alone never could.” Farmer to farmer While these virtual tours are available to any World Dairy Expo attendee, they provide a unique and valuable experience for farmers to openly collaborate in an inviting atmosphere. Langrehr describes the open discussion forum between producers as one of the most valuable aspects of the farm tours. “Although these virtual farm tours have the name virtual in them, the core part of it is that producer-to-producer connection,” Langrehr says. “That’s why it’s gone on for so long.” The farm tours connect farmers with individuals that understand what they are going through and who also want to improve their operations. Johnson says dairy farmers are especially curious about seeing how neighboring dairies operate. During virtual farm tours, farmers can see how other dairies are operating, learn what technologies other producers implement and gain inspiration for their own operations. Twenty-five years later, the virtual farm tours at World Dairy Expo continue to serve as a connection and educational avenue, inspiring curiosity, authenticity and meaningful conversations among the dairy industry.
