Marketing opportunities for beef-on-dairy calves Producers have become more sophisticated in evaluating reproductive decisions. Breeding strategies are no longer simply about producing enough replacements or creating a salable beef calf. They are becoming about matching specific market opportunities and delivering cattle that perform throughout the beef production chain. That shift is driving interest in in vitro fertilization (IVF) and embryo technologies. In addition to conventional beef-on-dairy matings, some producers are exploring ways to create more targeted genetic outcomes through value-added programs. The goal is greater control over the type of calf produced and, ultimately, the value that calf delivers to the beef market. New research from Texas Tech University underscores just how much those decisions matter.  Researchers found significant differences in growth performance and carcass characteristics across genetic combinations, reinforcing the idea that reproductive decisions on dairy farms can influence profitability throughout the beef supply chain. Advertisement From reproductive management to reproductive strategy Historically, dairy reproductive programs have focused on generating enough replacement heifers while maximizing reproductive efficiency. However, many herds require fewer replacements due to improved genetics, sexed semen and enhanced herd longevity. This shift creates an opportunity to think more strategically about the remaining pregnancies. Dairy producers are increasingly asking, “What type of pregnancy creates the greatest value for my operation?” A targeted reproductive program recognizes that not every cow serves the same purpose within the herd. Still, the goal is to maximize the value generated from every pregnancy while maintaining the right number of future replacements. As a result:
Elite animals may be candidates for IVF and embryo production.
Some cows may receive sexed semen.
Lower-priority animals may be bred to beef genetics.
IVF and embryo transfer technologies provide additional flexibility within this framework by allowing producers to create highly specific genetic combinations and place those embryos into recipient dairy cows. The importance of genetic alignment Recent research highlights why this alignment matters. The study evaluated more than 400 animals representing both straightbred beef genetics and beef-on-dairy crosses managed under commercial dairy and feedlot conditions. The analysis followed calves from the calf ranch through finishing and measured growth performance, feed intake and carcass characteristics. The findings demonstrated that genetics significantly influenced performance outcomes throughout the production cycle ( Figure 1 ). For example:
Charolais-sired cattle produced heavier birthweights, faster growth rates and heavier carcasses.
Charolais-Holstein cattle achieved the highest average daily gain and generated the heaviest hot carcass weights among the groups evaluated.
Angus-influenced cattle demonstrated advantages in marbling and carcass quality characteristics.
The research reinforced that dairy reproductive decisions have downstream consequences that extend far beyond calving. Sire breed, maternal genetics and management all contribute to differences in growth performance and carcass value. For dairy producers, this means selecting genetics should begin with the end market in mind. How embryos fit into the equation Embryos can help producers apply this concept in a practical way. Rather than relying solely on beef semen mated to dairy cows, beef embryos created from selected beef genetics and transferred into dairy recipients enable producers to capture the benefits of a beef pregnancy while incorporating a maternal genetic package designed specifically for beef performance. By utilizing IVF-generated embryos, producers gain greater control over genetic outcomes and can potentially create calves that better align with specific market objectives. Understanding your customer The beef-on-dairy marketplace continues to evolve, and one message from this research stands out – dairy producers play an increasingly important role in the beef value chain. Feedlots and packers continue to place greater emphasis on consistency, growth potential and carcass characteristics. This creates an opportunity for dairy producers to position themselves as preferred suppliers by delivering calves with predictable genetic merit and performance potential. The concept is straightforward, but important: Know your market. If a buyer prioritizes carcass weight and yield, genetics may be selected accordingly. If quality grade and marbling are rewarded, a different genetic package may be more appropriate. Reproductive decisions can then be aligned with those specific objectives. IVF and embryo technologies provide another tool for achieving that alignment. Looking ahead The next generation of dairy reproductive management may be defined by precision rather than volume. Pregnancy rates will always matter, but the economic value of those pregnancies may become equally important. Producers who strategically match genetics to business goals will be better positioned to capture opportunities across both the dairy and beef sectors. Beef embryos and IVF technology represent one pathway toward that objective. By creating targeted genetic outcomes and aligning reproductive decisions with downstream market demands, producers can move beyond simply producing calves and focus on producing calves that deliver greater value throughout the production chain. In an industry where margins are increasingly tied to efficiency and market alignment, every pregnancy has the potential to become a strategic investment.  Those who recognize this opportunity may find themselves better equipped to meet the demands of tomorrow’s beef-on-dairy marketplace.
