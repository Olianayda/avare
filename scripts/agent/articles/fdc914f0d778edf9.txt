Home &raquo; Blogs &raquo; Idaho Ag Proud | From the Editor &raquo; The people along the way Idaho Ag Proud The people along the way Cassidy Woolsey October 8, 2026 I love this month’s cover story because it reminds me of people who invested in me. Cassidy Woolsey Managing Editor / Ag Proud – Idaho Cassidy Woolsey serves as managing editor for Ag Proud – Idaho, covering agriculture across the state. She graduated from Utah State University with a bachelor’s degree in agricultural communications and journalism and spent eight years as an editor for Progressive Cattle before her current role. Email Cassidy Woolsey I’m sure we can all think of somebody, or many people, who gave us confidence, taught us skills and helped us in our journey to adulthood. Likewise, we can probably think of those who may have set us back a few steps – or a lot of steps – made us carry some wounds or gave us untrue perceptions of ourselves. Both are equally shaping. Aside from my parents, who deserve a lot of the credit for the person I am today, there have been people, experiences, programs and groups that gave me a place to belong, a chance to try something new or a little guidance when I needed it. I think about the people and businesses who bought my market show steers year after year, helping me pay for college. I think of my FFA advisor, who took a reserved teenager like me and pushed me to compete in prepared public speaking and other events that terrified me. I think of my basketball and volleyball coaches, who pushed my athletic skills and taught me to dig deep, even when I thought nothing was left. And I think of a college professor who introduced me to Progressive Cattle magazine, the magazine I originally started with, and planted the seed that maybe one day “I” could work for such a publication. Each of these – and many others – have shaped me or taught me important skills throughout my life. And so, as I spent an hour or so at Stoddard Farms in Grace, taking pictures and visiting with students participating in the area’s potato harvest, I couldn’t help but think that for some, this is one of those moments that matter. Advertisement Maybe this is their first time on a farm and they develop an appreciation for the work that gets their food from the farm to their plate. Maybe they experience long days between work and sports practice and find they can do more than they thought they could. Or maybe the farm gives them special responsibility and a boost of confidence that goes with that. As you read this month’s cover story , I hope you can recall the people and experiences in your youth that were more meaningful than you realized at the time. And I hope you take time to be that for the youth in your area. Take someone under your wing. Notice their achievements. Support youth programs and give back when you can.
