How to turn data into usable information Terrell Miller October 9, 2026 The goal of recordkeeping isn't to generate more reports and keep track of more details … it's to make better, more informed decisions. Most cattle producers understand the importance of keeping records. They've spent substantial time, effort and resources to collect the information. But once those records are entered, many operations never look at them again until tax season, weaning or a breed association report is due. The real value comes from using those records to make better decisions. Good records don’t just simply document what happened. The goal should be to help determine what happens next. Start with the question you're trying to answer When it's time to review records, the tendency may be to jump straight into reports or even reviewing individual records. Instead, start by asking a management question. Examples may include: Which replacement heifers should you keep? Is a certain bull contributing to high-quality calves? Which cows are consistently calving late or require extra attention? Once you know the question you're trying to answer, the right information becomes much easier to find. The goal isn't to generate more reports and keep track of more details … it's to make better, more informed decisions. Advertisement Look for trends instead of individual records Individual animal records are important, but the biggest opportunities often come from identifying and recognizing patterns among the herd. A single weaning weight may not mean much on its own. But if calves from one sire group consistently weigh less than others, that's a trend worth looking into further. One difficult calving may simply be bad luck or simply an outlier. Several difficult calvings within the same breeding group may indicate something that deserves immediate attention or certainly before the next breeding season. Looking for trends enables producers to spot and address issues before they become bigger, more extensive problems. Focus on the metrics that matter most Modern technology makes it easier than ever to collect more details. Though more data can be collected, it doesn’t mean that every measurement or piece of data deserves equal attention. Rather than trying to monitor many data points and measurements, it’s good to focus on several key measures that reflect the goals of your ranch. Examples may include pregnancy percentage, calving distribution, weaning weights, average daily gain, treatment rates, cow longevity and more. These details provide a snapshot of your herd’s overall performance while keeping the review process manageable. Looking at core measurements each year makes it easier to identify gradual improvements or recognize problems before they become larger ones. Turn information into action Records only create value when they lead to action. Sometimes the best decision is confirming that your current management is working well. Other times, the records point toward opportunities to improve efficiency, reduce costs or increase productivity. Either way, the information becomes much more valuable than simply serving as a history of past events. Review records throughout the year Perhaps one of the biggest mistakes producers make is waiting until the end of the year to look at their records. You’ll have many more benefits if you use them as part of your management practices throughout the various stages of the production cycle. Periodic reviews don't need to take lots of time. Even just a few scheduled reviews throughout the year can help identify opportunities while there's still time to act on them. Small improvements add up Good recordkeeping is not about collecting data just to check off something from your to-do list. It's about turning information into practical management decisions that improve the productivity and profitability of the ranch. Records tell you what happened. The real opportunity comes from using them to help decide what you should do next.
