Healthy hooves start with clean floors, automation can help Dan Schreiner September 17, 2026 By reducing cows' exposure to manure through automated manure removal, producers can help prevent hoof disease, improve cow comfort and mobility, and support healthier, more productive herds. Healthy hooves are the foundation of a productive dairy herd. Every day, dairy cows walk hundreds of yards between the feedbunk, freestalls and milking system, making clean surfaces essential for mobility, comfort and long-term performance. While routine hoof trimming, proper nutrition and footbath programs remain critical components of hoof care, automating manure removal can help. Manure-covered alleys expose hooves to bacteria associated with infectious diseases such as digital dermatitis (DD) while softening the hoof horn, increasing the likelihood of lameness. Manure is also tracked into stalls, into bedding, and eventually reaching udders and teat ends, creating additional herd health concerns. Clean floors support healthy cows Research and on-farm experience continue to show that cleaner cows are healthier cows. Cows that walk comfortably spend more time eating, resting and expressing their natural behaviors. They are more likely to maintain milk production, reproduce efficiently and remain productive in the herd for more lactations. Conversely, lameness can reduce feed intake, decrease milk yield, impair fertility and increase culling rates, making it a costly health challenge on dairy farms. DD, or hairy heel warts, is an extremely painful condition that reduces the cow's ability to walk to and from milking or the feedbunk and is the leading cause of lameness in cows. University of Wisconsin School of Veterinary Medicine research supports the negative impacts of DD on health, milk production and reproductive performance during the first lactation of a heifer diagnosed with DD pre-calving. The cost of DD can range between $3 and $239, based on individual first-lactation milk yield, per heifer. Based on a study from 2015, 45.6% of heifers having a DD event during the first lactation had at least one DD event before calving and increased to 67.6% of heifers during their first lactation if they had two or more DD events before calving. Cleaning where it counts Because manure plays a significant role in the spread of infectious hoof diseases, maintaining consistently clean walking surfaces is one of the most effective preventive management practices. Not every area of the barn requires the same level of attention. Behind the freestalls, where cows spend much of their time standing, entering and exiting stalls, manure accumulates much more rapidly than along the feedbunk. Traditional scraping schedules often clean the entire barn at fixed intervals, regardless of where manure is accumulating. Automated manure collection systems, however, allow producers to clean high-traffic areas more frequently while minimizing disruptions to cows. This targeted approach keeps walking surfaces cleaner throughout the day and reduces the amount of manure cows encounter as they move through the barn. Benefits for every dairy Although automated manure collection is commonly associated with automated milking systems, conventional dairies can see many of the same benefits as well. By timing cleaning cycles around milking schedules, producers can return cows from the parlor to freshly cleaned alleys. This is particularly valuable because teat ends remain open immediately after milking, making cleaner walking surfaces beneficial during this vulnerable period. Reducing labor while improving consistency Manure removal is one of the most labor-intensive daily tasks on a dairy farm. Manual scraping requires significant time every day. It is time that could otherwise be spent managing cows or reproduction, maintaining equipment or other high-value activities. We often recommend that producers use a stopwatch to track how much time manual manure removal takes. Automation provides consistency that is difficult to achieve manually. Rather than cleaning only when labor is available, automated systems can remove manure throughout the day, maintaining cleaner conditions around the clock while reducing repetitive labor demands. Building the foundation for long-term performance Improving hoof health isn't about any single management practice. It is about creating an environment that allows cows to move comfortably every day. By investing in automated manure removal, you can provide consistent, around-the-clock cleaning that supports hoof health, reduces labor demands and creates a healthier environment for cows.
