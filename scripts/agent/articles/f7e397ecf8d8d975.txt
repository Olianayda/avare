Basics of trading grain futures and options Pat Hatzenbuehler and Xiaoli Etienne September 4, 2026 Idaho wheat farmers are increasingly exploring futures and options to manage marketing risk, with University of Idaho Extension offering guidance to help producers understand costs, risks and trading basics. Farmers are continually interested in identifying new ways to improve their profitability and reduce risk, especially in the challenging market environment that has been observed over the past couple of years of declining crop sales prices and increasing input costs. One activity that many farmers are considering engaging in as part of their grain marketing strategy is trading grain futures and options contracts. Xiaoli Etienne Professor and Idaho Wheat Commission Endowed Chair in Commodity Risk Management / University of Idaho Email Xiaoli Etienne Pat Hatzenbuehler Extension Specialist – Crops Economics / University of Idaho Email Pat Hatzenbuehler Related The importance of a grain marketing plan Market Talk: Grain markets: The moving target of risk management Golden piles of grain To gain a better sense of the extent to which Idaho farmers are participating in trading grain futures and options, several of the authors were part of a research team that used funding from the USDA and Idaho Wheat Commission (IWC) to implement a survey in 2024 that focused on determining the strategies that Idaho wheat farmers are currently using to manage marketing risk. The data showed that 23% of Idaho wheat producers have traded futures and options contracts. So while many Idaho wheat farmers have used futures and options contracts as part of their risk management strategy, a majority still do not. We observed barriers to participation in trading futures and options while implementing grain marketing workshops in several locations in Idaho in collaboration with the IWC. These included uncertainty about the cost and risk implications and the specific logistical steps needed to participate in trading grain futures and options. To help reduce this uncertainty, our team of two faculty members and a graduate student in the University of Idaho (U of I) Department of Agricultural Economics and Rural Sociology collaborated on a recently published U of I Extension Bulletin (BUL 1135) titled “Basics of Trading Grain Futures and Options.” In the bulletin, we outline and answer key questions that have come up in recent grain marketing workshops about trading grain futures and options. In this article, we describe the frequently asked questions and define some key terms associated with trading grain futures and options. Frequently asked questions The frequently asked questions that we address in the bulletin include:
Why would farmers use futures and options contracts?
What futures exchanges manage grain futures and options markets?
What do I need to do to trade futures and options?
What are the cost and risk considerations?
Who trades futures and options?
How does it work for a farmer to trade a futures or options contract?

There are challenges in starting anything new, and it is sometimes difficult to know where to start. We hope that our bulletin will be helpful in determining whether trading grain futures and options is worth pursuing and, if so, that it will serve as a useful guide for identifying the main initial steps to do so. In addition to the information described in this article, we have a list of references under “Further Reading” that provide greater detail on specific aspects of trading grain futures and options that may also be useful.
