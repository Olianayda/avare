Total digestible nutrients (TDN) is the most common method to describe how much energy (i.e., calories) is in a feed. The best supplement option for each situation should be based on supplementation needs, feeding frequency considerations and cost per pound of TDN or crude protein. Unfortunately, the TDN content of a feed is not on the feed tag. Crude fiber is listed on the feed tag and can be an indicator of the TDN content of feeds with multiple ingredients. In general, the TDN content decreases as crude fiber goes up . Although they may cost more per bag, buying supplements with a higher TDN content (less crude fiber) will almost always result in lower total feed costs. What about the crude fat content? It is important to realize that the digestibility of crude fat is less than the digestibility of most carbohydrates found in concentrate feeds. Additionally, having too much fat in the diet can reduce fiber digestibility and intake. If the actual TDN content of a feed is not available, crude fiber is generally a better indicator of the energy content of a feed than crude fat. Range cubes and other supplements can be grouped into three general categories based on their targeted use. Advertisement
Protein supplements : This group consists of feeds such as cottonseed meal, soybean meal and cubes that contain around 38% to 40% protein.
Protein and energy supplements : This group consists of feeds such as DDG (dried distillers grains), corn gluten feed and 20% protein cubes. This group is best used when both protein and energy need to be added to the diet. Depending on the supplier, DDG can be purchased loose or as a cube.
Energy supplements : This group includes feeds such as whole shell corn, soybean hull pellets and 11% to 14% protein cubes. These feeds are best used when energy is the primary nutrient lacking in the diet.
Some feed companies make multiple versions of cubes with the same or similar crude protein content. For example, they may have four different 20% crude protein cubes. These cubes will vary in TDN content and type of crude protein (natural protein or equivalent crude protein from non-protein nitrogen). When evaluating 20% cubes, it is desirable for the crude fiber to be 11% or preferably less. In forage-based diets, natural protein is generally preferred to non-protein nitrogen.
