The second half of 2026 has begun with headwinds facing U.S. dairy product exports. For another consecutive month, year-over-year dairy product exports fell 5% in July as products like cheese remained in a steadfast export market and others like butterfat stumbled against global competition. Email Jenn Coyne Related Dairy export value at 5-year high, but U.S. ag trade deficit grows U.S. Dairy Export Council: Another record dairy export year December concludes year of limited dairy export growth in 2023 Yet, as the year-over-year comparison lacks enthusiasm, largely based on a strong 2025 performance, year-to-date volume and value of dairy product exports are still exciting, as described in the latest U.S. Dairy Export Council’s (USDEC) trade data analysis . With July’s data in, year-over-year export value has sprung 16% to $984 million, and year-to-date export volume in milk solid equivalent terms is up 9% with value up 12%. Cheese is the steady hand in turbulent export market U.S. cheese remained the true constant in July’s dairy export performance – as it has month over month for some time now – with exports rising 25% to 65,256 metric tons from July of last year. This volume marks a new monthly record and is the result of all major destinations boosting imports by double digits. For instance, Mexico imported 28% more U.S. cheese, up 4,914 metric tons than the same month a year prior. South Korea was up 34% or 2,052 metric tons, Australia was up 59% or 2,006 metric tons, and South America was up 70% or 1,553 metric tons. USDEC noted that smaller markets grew similarly. Even as the United Arab Emirates recorded the largest year-over-year decline in U.S. cheese imports, down 86% or 427 metric tons, the product still made it to the region. Exports to Saudi Arabia grew 89% year over year in July, up 713 metric tons, which lifted U.S. cheese exports to the Middle East by 19%, or 327 metric tons. U.S. butterfat exports also rose for another consecutive month – carrying on a growth streak that began in May 2024. Yet, the growth was barely there, up just 1%, or 166 metric tons, since July of last year and was solely due to the performance of anhydrous milkfat, up 23%, as butter exports fell 8%. The year-over-year lackluster performance is due to incredibly strong prior-year performances that are set as the benchmark and, perhaps more importantly, is the growing global supply of butter coupled with an easing price advantage for the U.S. dairy product. Advertisement Low-protein whey also had a strong month in July as year-over-year exports improved 19%, up 8,419 metric tons. USDEC stated shipments of the product remain scorching hot. However, other whey categories struggled, particularly as processors continue to meet the demand of U.S. consumers and their hunger for higher-protein products. As a result, low-protein whey protein concentrate (WPC) exports were down 28% in July, or 3,719 metric tons. Lastly, nonfat dry milk/skim milk powder products continued to struggle in the export market in July, falling 19% year over year. That’s 11,325 metric tons. This was simply the result of U.S. supply and high prices compared to the global marketplace. August NEXT-assisted export sales surpass 16 million pounds The National Milk Producers Federation (NMPF) Export and Trade (NEXT) member cooperatives added 16.9 million pounds of product to 2026 sales through the month of August. The program secured 57 contracts in the month with products going to 16 countries in Asia, North America, Middle East-North Africa, Oceania, the Caribbean, South America and Central America. Product was shipped in August and will continue to be moved through July 2027. Year-to-date contracts have committed nearly 210 million pounds of U.S. product. Of that, U.S. cheese has risen to the top with 82 million pounds of product secured in NEXT-assisted sales for 2026 so far. U.S. replacements rise as embryos retreat U.S. dairy heifer replacement exports rebounded in July, up threefold from June’s staggering 27 animal units to 109 animal units. Canada remained the top purchaser of U.S. dairy heifer replacement animals, importing 97 live cattle. The remaining dozen went to Brazil, their first imports since February. However, another month went by with Mexico not purchasing any U.S. dairy heifer replacement exports. July’s replacement heifer sales bring the year-to-date total exports to 1,247 animal units. The volume continues to surpass 2025 levels. By the end of July last year, only 770 U.S. dairy heifer replacements were exported, most of which went south of the border to Mexico.  On the contrary, U.S. dairy embryo exports continued to fall in July. Exports were down 5% from the previous month to 461 units. Spain, Germany and Japan were the top importers, purchasing 131 units, 126 units and 112 units, respectively. Although Germany’s imports were down 17% from June and the lowest number of U.S. embryos purchased since April. July’s U.S. dairy embryo exports bring the total to 2,653 units year to date, on similar pace with last year’s volume. Over the same period in 2025, U.S. dairy embryo exports were at 2,692 units. Hay export markets improve from June U.S. dairy-quality alfalfa hay exports rebounded in July with the top three destinations all increasing purchases of the U.S. forage. In July, U.S. dairy-quality alfalfa hay exports rose 15% to 129,321 metric tons. China not only remained the No. 1 purchasers of the U.S. product, but increased volume to 54,896 metric tons, up 35% from June. Additionally, Japan imported 32,460 metric tons of U.S. dairy-quality alfalfa hay, up 3% from the month prior, and South Korea imported 18,093 metric tons of the forage, up 25%. Strong export volumes to key destinations improved the year-to-date total of U.S. dairy-quality alfalfa hay exports. In the first seven months of the year, exports of this forage were at 983,010 metric tons. The market for other U.S. hay exports was not as exciting but also did not disappoint. In July, the total metric tons of forage exported was 64,379 metric tons, up 113 metric tons but not enough to change the volume on a percentage basis. As the top importers – Japan, South Korea and Taiwan – remained relatively consistent in their purchases, smaller markets, such as Canada and the United Arab Emirates, grew by 59% and 26%, respectively. With July’s data, year-to-date volume of other U.S. hay exports sits at 523,406 metric tons. Trade deficit expands in July July’s U.S. agricultural trade deficit widened to $2.91 billion. Exports in July were recorded at $14.51 billion, and imports were at $17.42 billion according to the Department of Commerce/Census Bureau. The fiscal year-to-date (Oct. 1, 2025, to Sept. 30, 2026) balance is a deficit of $18.24 million.
