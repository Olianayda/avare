National FFA Organization endorses dairy cattle judging contest at World Dairy Expo Patryce Eldredge LeFevre September 18, 2026 The National FFA Organization endorses the dairy cattle judging contest at World Dairy Expo as an event members can participate in. Judging contests have been time-honored events at World Dairy Expo, but this year, the dairy judging contest received extra attention. This contest has now been endorsed by the National FFA Organization, receiving its stamp of approval. This partnership put the event on the radar for chapters across the nation and expanded the opportunities members have to participate in the organization. Patryce Eldredge LeFevre Editorial Intern / Progressive Dairy Related World Dairy Expo 2019: New products, technologies to be introduced at World Dairy Expo World Dairy Expo 2018: Seminars and virtual tours presented daily at World Dairy Expo Explore new products showcased at World Dairy Expo 2025 From mind to materiality: How this partnership came to be When Dakota Bockenhauer, the superintendent for the FFA contests, stepped into his role with World Dairy Expo, he wanted to learn more about the organization and researched the relationship between World Dairy Expo and FFA. “Long story short, this contest was happening, and it was designed for the FFA program, but as far as the National FFA, they didn’t really know these contests existed,” Bockenhauer says. Lexa Miller, the coordinator for World Dairy Expo’s cattle show and contests, says the judging contest was once called the National FFA Contest. Curious about the name, Miller looked into its history and discovered it was not actually a national contest. Instead, "national" was meant to indicate the contest was open to FFA chapters across the U.S. The committee eventually removed national from the title and renamed it the Central FFA Contest. The change, however, caused some confusion. Chapters farther away asked whether the contest was still open to them, which it was. As a result, the committee later dropped the word central from the name as well. Advertisement Around the time of those name changes, Miller says the National FFA Organization reached out, and the puzzle pieces came together. “We've invited the National FFA and their CDE [career development event] leaders to come up this year to the contest, check things out and partake in the contest and World Dairy Expo for themselves,” Bockenhauer says. “We're hoping to grow this partnership into the other aspects of the contest.” “One of the biggest benefits of these contests is bringing students and industry professionals together. Those connections can open doors to internships, mentorships, higher education opportunities and future careers,” Miller says. “The endorsement elevates the contest’s national recognition and creates valuable opportunities for students to develop skills that prepare them for careers in the dairy industry.” Sandra Cordes, Manawa FFA chapter advisor and former contest participant, says it is wonderful that the FFA recognizes the contest as a legitimate way for students to participate and learn. As an FFA advisor responsible for helping her chapter learn and grow through the organization, she likes knowing it is something the National FFA Organization supports. Benefits beyond awards are among the purpose of the contest While this year is the first time the contest was endorsed, its purpose has historically been the same: to help participants learn and develop their skills. “After talking with coaches from across the United States, it has become very apparent that students who participate in all judging contests develop strong communication, critical thinking and leadership skills. These students often stand out when applying for jobs or pursuing higher education because they have already built confidence in speaking and presenting themselves professionally,” Miller says. “I believe FFA is an excellent organization for developing those skills early on through the many activities, competitions and leadership opportunities it offers. Those experiences help prepare students for success in college and throughout their careers.” Cordes says the evaluation contests have several embedded life skills. She says that whether you are picking out a pair of jeans or deciding on your first job, you need to be able to assess the qualities of something and decide what is most important to you, creating a rubric for what you place value on. Youth learn to find their voice through reason classes and learn how to defend their decisions. “FFA's statement is that we are always striving to grow leaders, build communities and strengthen agriculture,” Cordes says. “I think the contest plays into all three of those very well because being an individual as well as a team member and how you go about preparing for your team fits into those categories. It’s a chance to be able to network, to be able to meet individuals that are in the industry, to be able to represent your school as well as your community and agriculture. I think it fits very well with that.” Chapter attendance at the contest While the contest was open to chapters across the nation, attendance has mainly been local, representing chapters from the Midwestern states such as Wisconsin, Illinois, Iowa and Minnesota. However, chapters from farther away, including California, Kentucky and Pennsylvania, have also participated. Those chapters tend to be contestants who are showing their own cattle at the event, are highly competitive in these evaluation-type events and have funding to help make the trip. “World Dairy Expo is a worldwide event, but sadly, on the FFA side, it’s currently somewhat limited to our Midwest chapters that are local,” Bockenhauer says. “We’re hoping this endorsement by the National FFA Organization will support that and put this out to FFA members nationwide and help promote this opportunity.” The endorsement has the potential to improve FFA member attendance. Bockenhauer says total FFA attendance at World Dairy Expo contests has averaged about 800 to 900 participants. At the time of print, Bockenhauer hoped to see about 1,000 FFA members participate this year with the added FFA endorsement of the dairy judging contest. “We're just excited on both fronts being able to represent FFA as well as World Dairy Expo and make a partnership and continue to build on something that's already great,” Bockenhauer says.
