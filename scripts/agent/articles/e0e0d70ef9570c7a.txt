Home &raquo; Blogs &raquo; Progressive Cattle Canada From the Editor &raquo; Moments are the gifts to remember at Thanksgiving Moments are the gifts to remember at Thanksgiving David Cooper August 28, 2026 When the seasons turn from hot to pleasantly cool, painting the landscape with a palette of warm amber colours of autumn, your body begins to absorb change in a good way. Perhaps that’s why the Thanksgiving season is a welcome reprieve from the searing annual stress of summer. David Cooper Managing Editor / Progressive Cattle Email David Cooper In Canada, where the cooler climate helps bring about that transition a bit earlier than in the States, that change of seasons ushers in the holiday of fond appreciation on the second Monday of October. Speaking as an American, I am somewhat jealous Canada gets to experience it sooner than we do. Fall is harvest time, when our work and efforts in the planting season begin to pay off. In cattle production, the winter or spring calves are preconditioned and weaned, now on their way to a stocker or feeder. For many in the cow-calf segment, the hard work may feel like it’s done. As those vibrant colours on trees go from ivy green to shades of crimson, copper, saffron, gold, mustard yellow and bright lemon yellow; and the prairies of Saskatchewan have matured from sage green to butter yellow, hopefully your mind and body can recognize what God has granted through change – and by seeing everything that can bring out our gratitude. It’s been a busy year for my family. Three of our kids were married in an 11-month span, all concluding in September. Sure, there is relief that the stress is gone. But there will be more stress to come, for my kids and for us. It’s like the story of a new bride telling her mother, “Thank goodness, I’m at the end of my problems.” The mother replied, “Yes, dear. But which end?” Advertisement If you don’t take the time to savour each year’s blessings and challenges, you miss the chance to grow from the seasons past, present and future. I am grateful for everything I missed in the last year. Sickness, unemployment, the loss of a child. I saw others survive these same struggles, and others go through natural disasters such as wildfires, earthquakes and floods. It’s staggering to think how others can pull through their hardest chapters and yet find the strength to rebuild, after such heartbreak. But I’m just as grateful for moments I didn’t miss. The chances to say goodbye to those ready to pass on. Walking a daughter down an aisle to her groom. Seeing my son start a new business. Helping move another son and his wife as she begins law school. Watching my youngest start his last year of high school. I’m grateful for friendships newly made. To see each other differently than how society would define us. To understand different perspectives and challenges they met. But I’m just as grateful for my friendships rekindled. Where there was absence, there is new connection. Where there was misunderstanding, there is now forgiveness. The season of thanks offers multiple chances to acknowledge all the people, places, moments and memories we have gained in a year, or in a lifetime. May your opportunities this Thanksgiving be appreciated and lead to more challenges and blessings on the road ahead.
