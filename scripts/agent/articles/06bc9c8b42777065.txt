The USDA’s monthly World Ag Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE) report was released Oct. 9. Based on the latest Milk Production report, cow inventories were lowered for 2026, and output per cow is lowered for both 2026 and 2027. At 236.9 billion pounds, the 2026 milk production estimate was lowered from last month’s report, but still up 5.2 billion from 2025’s total of 231.7 billion pounds. For 2026 and 2027, butter and cheese price forecasts are decreased based on recent prices and growth in butterfat production. The nonfat dry milk (NDM) and whey price forecasts are raised to reflect recent prices and strong demand for protein products. As a result, the Class III price forecast is lowered to $15.80 per hundredweight (cwt) as higher whey prices only partially offset lower cheese prices, and the Class IV price is raised to $18.90 cwt as higher NDM prices more than fully offset lower butter prices. The 2026 all-milk price is unchanged at $19.90 per cwt. In its forecast for 2027, the USDA estimated milk production at 238.4 billion pounds, down 0.2 billion pounds from last month. For 2027, the Class III price forecast is lowered to $16.90 per cwt, and the Class IV price forecast is increased to $18.90 cwt. The all-milk price forecast for 2027 is raised 65 cents to $20.45 per cwt. Beef outlook For 2026, beef production lowered for the second half as lower expected steer and heifer slaughter and lighter carcass weights more than offset higher cow slaughter. For 2027, beef production is lowered on lighter dressed weights as well as lower-than-expected feedlot placements in the second half of 2026 that are expected to slow marketings during the first quarter of 2027. Advertisement Fed cattle prices were unchanged for 2026 and 2027. The 2025 average was forecast at $224.37 per cwt, while the 2026 average is now forecast at $237.35 per cwt, with highest prices in the second quarter of the year. Feed supply, price forecasts The USDA’s WASDE and Crop Production reports provided potential insights into dairy feedstuff supplies and prices: Corn This month’s WASDE 2026-27 U.S. corn outlook is for larger supplies, exports, domestic use and ending stocks. Corn production is higher, up 234 million bushels to 16 billion on a 2.7-bushel increase in yield to 181.2 bushels per acre. Harvested area for grain is unchanged at 88.5 million acres. Total use is increased 125 million bushels to 16.3 billion. Each component of use is increased. Exports are raised 25 million bushels to 3.3 billion on increased supplies. With supply increased more than use, ending stocks rise by 282 million bushels to 1.8 billion. At $4.70 per bushel, the projected season-average corn price received by producers is lowered a dime. It is also up 55 cents from the 2024-25 average of $4.15 per bushel. Soybeans This month’s 2026-27 soybean outlook includes higher production compared to last month. Soybean production is forecast at 4.56 billion bushels, up 27 million on higher yields. Harvested area is unchanged at 85.9 million acres. The soybean yield is projected at 53.1 bushels per acre, up 0.3 bushels from the September forecast. Soybean supplies are raised 17 million bushels to 4.9 billion on higher production partly offset by lower beginning stocks. With a 10 million-bushel increase to exports and unchanged crush, ending stocks are up 5 million bushels to 315 million. The 2026-27 U.S. season-average soybean price forecast is unchanged at $12 per bushel, compared with $10.40 in 2025-26. The soybean meal price is also unchanged at $340 per short ton, and the soybean oil prices was unchanged at 70 cents per pound. Cottonseed The 2026-27 U.S. cotton forecasts shows higher production and ending stocks compared to last month. All cotton production forecast is increased 2% to 13.44 million bales as the projected national average yield is also raised 2% to 790 pounds per harvested acre. Larger crops for all Delta states drive the increase, with changes in other states largely offsetting each other. With the larger U.S. crop, the forecast for ending stocks is raised 6% to 3.8 million bales, for a stocks-to-use ratio of 27.5%, up from 26.1% last month. The 2025-26 season-average farm price forecast is raised marginally to 62.2 cents per pound reflecting year-end trade data from census and other sources. The projected season-average price for 2026-27 is lowered a penny to 77 cents per pound. Alfalfa and other hay The latest USDA Ag Prices report indicated dairy-quality alfalfa hay prices averaged $243 per ton in August, while alfalfa hay prices averaged $206 per ton, and prices for other hay averaged $152 per ton.
