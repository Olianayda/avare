The kid who used to run off the school bus to his grandparents' barn is now the Klussendorf Association's 19th honorary member. Six decades on, Peter Vail's greatest work isn't the banners. It's the people he brought along with him.

Peter Vail was making his way onto the colored shavings at World Dairy Expo when the citation got to the heart of it.
The Klussendorf Award, it said, represents "the highest ideals of our industry: character, sportsmanship, integrity, dedication, and an unwavering commitment to excellence in the dairy show ring." Few individuals, it went on, have "embodied these values as completely or influenced as many people as this native New Yorker."
Vail, of Salem, N.Y., is 87. He has owned or bred champions in Holsteins, Jerseys, Brown Swiss, Ayrshires and Milking Shorthorns. His partnership bred and sold the most expensive Brown Swiss cow in U.S. history. But the story he told during Expo week was about a calf.
A young woman from Vermont had come up to him and asked if she could lease one for next year. Vail said yes. He ran her through what he had in September calves and December calves, and told her to pick one.
"You take the calf and lease the calf," he told Casey Treloar. "It's fine with me."
That's Peter Vail. Now the Klussendorf Association has made it official.
"It's just incredibly humbling," he said of joining a roll that has been honoring the show ring's best people since 1937.
From a 1964 Dispersal to a Three-Generation Fertilizer Business
The citation took the crowd back to the beginning. At 12, Vail would run off the school bus and straight across the road to his grandparents' farm in eastern New York. He and his brother Walter showed through 4-H. As teenagers, they rode the train to Harrisburg, Pa., and Waterloo, Iowa, fitting their own cattle and other people's.
At Cornell, he made the dairy judging team under Dr. George Trimberger, the man the citation called legendary. To this day, Vail credits Trimberger with training his eye for a good cow.
After graduation, he bought a small herd of quality Brown Swiss. He dispersed the herd in 1964. For a lot of young cattlemen, that would have been the end of the story.
For Vail, it was the start of the build.
He went into fertilizer. The company that became CaroVail traces its beginning to 1979 and now serves farms from locations across New York and Massachusetts, custom-blending fertilizer to each farm's soil. It's a business that lives on knowing your customer's land and earning that trust back every season.
"We've dealt with farmers that have treated us very, very well," Vail said. Three generations later, the family is still running it. "My son, grandson and granddaughter [are] involved in that business now."
CaroVail became the engine. When Vail returned to cattle, he invested millions of dollars and helped develop cattle in most every breed, particularly Brown Swiss, Ayrshire and Milking Shorthorn. He brought capital and a judge's eye, and he used both.
And he's done it with Lyn beside him. Peter and Lyn Vail have been married for more than 65 years. They have two children, Peter Jr. and Lauren.
Five Brown Swiss Grand Champions in Nine Years
"Peter has literally changed the game for investing in great cattle," Tim Abbott of Showbox Sires. "His standard of success both in the show ring, breeding world and financial success of his investment strategy are unmatched and set the standard in the business."
The proof starts in Copake, N.Y. In 2002, Vail and Ken Main formed Elite Dairy, and the Brown Swiss Association still describes their plan in seven words: "buy the best and make them better."
They did both. Over 16 years, animals owned or bred by Elite earned more than 40 All-American recognitions. Five different cows won Grand Champion Brown Swiss at World Dairy Expo for them: Old Mill JP Grace in 2010, Dublin-Hills Treats in 2011, Cutting Edge B Gretchen-ET in 2013, Eloc Wagor Kandid in 2014, and Cutting Edge T Delilah in 2018. Gretchen and Delilah both carried the partnership's own Cutting Edge prefix, and breeding Gretchen was the accomplishment both partners called their biggest.
Cutting Edge B Gretchen-ET wears the Grand Champion sash at World Dairy Expo 2013 after winning the four-year-old class for Ken Main and Peter Vail. She was also Grand at the Eastern National that year, and both partners called breeding her their biggest accomplishment.
By 2021, World Dairy Expo counted six Grand Champion titles and 13 straight years as Premier Exhibitor of the International Brown Swiss Show. That year, the Klussendorf Association named Main and Vail its 12th Robert "Whitey" McKown Master Breeders. This year, it honored Vail on his own.
A $210,000 Record and the Cow That Never Left
Ask Vail about his favorites, and he can't stop at one. He names one per breed instead: Griffin, Blexy, Delilah, Karmina, Willow and Jeanette.
"I think the Delilah cow and the Griffin cow would be the two cows that stuck with me the longest," he said.
Cutting Edge T Delilah wears the Supreme Champion blanket at World Dairy Expo 2018. In 2019, she sold for a U.S. Brown Swiss record of $210,000 and won Supreme again. She now has 77 registered offspring, and Peter Vail bought another IVF session out of her this summer.
Cutting Edge T Delilah was Supreme Champion of World Dairy Expo in 2018. On Sept. 4, 2019, she walked in to open the Elite Dairy dispersal and sold for $210,000 , the highest price ever paid for a U.S. Brown Swiss, according to the Brown Swiss Association . That was 23.5 times the sale's $8,948 average . A few weeks later, under new ownership, she won Supreme again .
Most people sell a cow for a breed record and take the win. Vail kept going.
"Delilah has 77 offspring registered," he said. "… I just bought an IVF session out of her this summer. So we're gonna do her again next week."
How many breeders have the discipline to keep investing in genetics they cashed out seven years ago? That isn't speculation. That's devotion to a cow family.
