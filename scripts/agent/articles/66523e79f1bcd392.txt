Chobani to establish its first dairy operation in Pennsylvania Government officials gathered to announce a historic $1.2 billion investment from Chobani to establish its first dairy product manufacturing operation in Pennsylvania. This project will create 900 full-time jobs over the next five years in the Lehigh Valley and greatly expand markets and opportunities for more than 4,000 dairy farmers in Pennsylvania. “I’ve spent a lot of time on our farms and in our rural communities, hearing directly from Pennsylvanians about the kind of investments they want to see in our Commonwealth. This 1.2 billion dollar investment from Chobani is the largest private sector investment in the history of Pennsylvania’s agriculture industry and will strengthen our dairy industry, support our farmers and reinforce our position as a national leader in agriculture and food manufacturing,” said Gov. Josh Shapiro. Chobani will assume operations of Keurig Dr Pepper’s (KDP) manufacturing facility, equipment and related infrastructure, where Chobani plans to manufacture innovative food products beyond yogurt. Advertisement When fully operational, the facility is expected to process more than 3 billion pounds of Pennsylvania milk annually – equivalent to approximately 30% of all milk currently produced in the Commonwealth – the largest increase in demand for Pennsylvania dairy in the state’s history. Production under Chobani is expected to begin in 2027. Secretary Rollins unveils plan to modernize agricultural data collection U.S. Secretary of Agriculture Brooke L. Rollins announced a data modernization plan to reduce unnecessary burdens on producers and improve the timeliness, accuracy and usefulness of the agricultural data that informs decisions across American agriculture. Since the USDA was established in 1862, collecting and sharing reliable agricultural information has been central to its mission. Today, USDA data informs crop and livestock estimates, risk management tools, disaster assistance and critical business decisions made by farmers, ranchers, agribusinesses, researchers and policymakers. In February, Rollins issued a request for information seeking feedback on the opportunities, challenges and emerging areas in USDA data. Additionally, Rollins and USDA leadership held a series of data modernization listening sessions at state fairs and farm shows across the country throughout August to hear directly from hundreds of producers and agricultural stakeholders. That feedback helped shape the USDA Data Modernization Plan and its four pillars: Modernize the data reporting experience The USDA will expand use of administrative data, improve mobile access to surveys, explore opportunities to use prefilled information where appropriate and strengthen coordination across the department. These changes will reduce duplicative requests and limit the amount of time producers spend reporting information. Enhance acreage and yield estimation The USDA will conduct a pilot to evaluate the use of improved satellite imagery, geospatial tools, crop models and other emerging technologies combined with essential producer-reported information to enhance acreage and yield estimations. The USDA will also assess the use of optional field inputs to enhance and ground truth yield estimates and continue advancing yield research through collaboration with agricultural partners and land-grant universities. Integrate data and technology platforms The USDA will modernize its technology infrastructure, expand secure data-sharing capabilities and evaluate the responsible use of tools such as artificial intelligence and machine learning. Better integrated systems will improve efficiency, reduce duplication and allow the USDA to produce more timely and useful information. Expand trust and transparency with producers The USDA will protect producer privacy, clearly explain why information is requested and how it is used, and create additional opportunities for producers to provide feedback. The USDA will also improve communication surrounding reports, methodologies, response rates and data limitations. The USDA will begin implementing actions under the plan immediately and will continue engaging farmers, ranchers and agricultural stakeholders throughout the process. Future expectations push farmer sentiment higher Farmer sentiment improved in for the second month in a row with the largest improvement coming from the future expectations, according to the latest Purdue University/CME Group Ag Economy Barometer . “Respondents were more optimistic about their financial prospects and land values in the upcoming year and exports in the next five years,” said Michael Langemeier, the barometer’s principal investigator and director of Purdue University’s Center for Commercial Agriculture. Despite the improvement in producer sentiment, important concerns remain. High input costs, low crop and livestock prices and rising interest rates are keeping producers cautious in current conditions. The Ag Economy Barometer provides a monthly snapshot of farmer sentiment regarding the state of the agricultural economy. The survey collects responses from 400 producers whose annual market value of production is equal to or exceeds $500,000. Minimum targets by enterprise are as follows: 53% corn/soybeans, 14% wheat, 3% cotton, 19% beef cattle, 5% dairy and 6% hogs. Latest survey results, released Sept. 1, reflect ag producer outlooks as of Aug. 10-14. GDT index up slightly again The price index of dairy product prices sold on the Global Dairy Trade (GDT) platform is up 0.9% in the auction held Sept. 1 . Compared to the previous auction, prices for individual product categories were mixed. Skim milk powder and buttermilk powder were up 5.3% and 4.6%, respectively. Lactose was up 2%, and mozzarella was up 0.3%. Cheddar cheese was down 6.6%. Anhydrous milkfat was down 1.3%. Butter and whole milk powder were down by less than 1%. The GDT platform offers dairy products from several global companies: Fonterra (New Zealand), Darigold, Valley Milk and Dairy America (U.S.), Inalpi (Italy), Arla (Denmark), Arla Foods Ingredients (Denmark), BMI (Germany), Kerry Dairy (Ireland) and Solarec (Belgium). The next GDT auction is Sept. 15. FSA interest rates continue to hold in September The announced interest rates on loans through the USDA’s Farm Service Agency (FSA) are unchanged this month. As we begin September 2026, interest rates for operating and ownership loans (compared to August) are as follows:
Farm operating loans (direct): 5.25%, unchanged
Farm ownership loans (direct): 6%, unchanged
Farm ownership loans (direct, joint financing): 4%, unchanged
Farm ownership loans (down payment): 2%, unchanged
Emergency loan (amount of actual loss): 3.75%, unchanged
The FSA also offers guaranteed loans through commercial lenders at rates set by those lenders. For more information, producers can contact their local USDA Service Center . SNAP dairy incentive program expands to Oklahoma A nutrition incentive program championed by the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) to help low-income families purchase healthy fluid milk products is expanding to Oklahoma. The Add Milk! program is being launched in grocery stores to provide a dollar-for-dollar match for participants in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, or SNAP, when they purchase low-fat or nonfat milk. A community event held at Oasis Fresh Market in Tulsa, Oklahoma, on Aug. 22, marked the launch of Add Milk! at three Oasis locations in the state. Add Milk! was established as part of the Healthy Fluid Milk Incentives (HFMI) program authorized in the 2018 Farm Bill to help SNAP households purchase more nutrient-dense milk products. With the Oklahoma launch, Add Milk! now reaches more than 1,200 stores across 32 states. Nearly 695,000 Oklahoma residents – about 1 in 6 people in the state – participate in SNAP. For those families, Add Milk! helps stretch monthly food benefits while making it easier to choose milk. Three organizations receive grants from Dairy’s Foundation Dairy’s Foundation has awarded grant funding to organizations in Texas, Virginia and Wisconsin to support programs focused on building healthy and vibrant dairy communities across the U.S. The foundation awards two rounds of grants each year. This round of grants, awarded in August 2026, will support:
Animal Agriculture Alliance, Arlington, Virginia, to support the College Aggies Online program that prepares college students to be more confident advocates for agriculture online and on campus through resources, mentorships and hands-on experiences
Farm Wisconsin Discovery Center, Manitowoc, Wisconsin, to purchase a fiberglass milking cow for a hands-on educational exhibit that will enhance visitor experiences at the center
G-Wiz Adventures, Fort Worth, Texas, to support the Dairy Stewardship and Trust Lab in developing curriculum and toolkits for producer-facing learning sessions and public trust field days focused on dairy stewardship, sustainability and animal care
The next deadline for grant applications is Dec. 1.  More information is available for grant seekers at Dairy Foundation's website .
