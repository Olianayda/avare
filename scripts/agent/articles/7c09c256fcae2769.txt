The USDA’s monthly World Ag Supply and Demand Estimates (WASDE) report was released Sept. 11. Based on the latest Milk Production report, both cow inventories and milk output per cow are raised for 2026 and 2027. 2026 milk At 237.2 billion pounds, the 2026 milk production estimate was raised from last month’s report and would be up 5.5 billion from 2025’s total of 231.7 billion pounds. For 2026, the price forecasts are decreased for butter and cheese but increased for nonfat dry milk (NDM) to reflect recent prices. Whey price forecasts are unchanged in 2026. As a result, the Class III price forecast is lowered to $16.20 per hundredweight (cwt) as higher whey prices only partially offset lower cheese prices, and the Class IV price is raised to $18.55 cwt as higher NDM prices more than fully offset lower butter prices. The 2026 all-milk price is raised a nickel to $19.90 per cwt. 2027 milk In its forecast for 2027, the USDA estimated milk production at 238.6 billion pounds, up 0.6 billion pounds from last month. Advertisement For 2027, the price forecasts are decreased for butter and cheese but increased for NDM to reflect recent prices. Whey price forecasts are increased in 2027. Consequently, the Class III price forecast is lowered to $17.10 per cwt, and the Class IV price forecast is increased to $17.55 cwt. The all-milk price forecast for 2027 is unchanged at $19.80 per cwt. Beef outlook For 2026, beef production is reduced on a slower pace of fed cattle marketings in the third quarter, lighter dressed weights in the third quarter and lower cow slaughter in the fourth quarter. For 2027, beef production is lowered on lighter dressed weights and lower than expected feedlot placements in the first half of 2027. Fed cattle prices are lowered for the third and fourth quarters of 2026 on lower than expected packer demand. Lower price forecasts are carried through the end of 2027. The 2025 average was forecast at $224.37 per cwt, while the 2026 average is now forecast at $237.35 per cwt, with highest prices in the second quarter of the year. Feed supply, price forecasts The USDA’s WASDE and Crop Production reports provided potential insights into dairy feedstuff supplies and prices: Corn This month’s WASDE 2026-27 U.S. corn outlook is for smaller supplies, lower domestic use, unchanged exports and reduced ending stocks. Corn production is forecast at 15.8 billion bushels, down 1% (213 million bushels) from last month and down 7% from last year. If realized, this would be the second-highest production for grain on record for the U.S. Based on conditions as of Sept. 1, the yield is forecast at 178.5 bushels per acre, down 2.2 bushels from the previous forecast and down 8 bushels from last year’s record-high 186.5 bushels per acre. Record-high yields are forecast in Arkansas, Iowa, Mississippi and Tennessee. At $4.80 per bushel, the projected season-average corn price received by producers is raised 30 cents. It is also up 65 cents from the 2024-25 average of $4.15 per bushel. Soybeans This month’s 2026-27 soybean outlook includes higher production and exports, and lower ending stocks compared to last month. Soybean production is projected at 4.5 billion bushels, up less than 1% (16 million bushels) from the previous estimate and up 6% from 2025. Total planted area, at 86.9 million acres, is up less than 1% from the previous estimate and up 7% from the previous year. Acreage harvested for beans is forecast at 85.9 million acres, up less than 1% from the previous forecast and up 7% from last year. The soybean yield of 52.8 bushels per acre is up 0.1 bushel from last month. If realized, this would be the second-highest yield on record for the nation. The crush forecast is unchanged, while the soybean export forecast is raised 25 million bushels to 1.69 billion. The 2026-27 U.S. season-average soybean price forecast is up 60 cents at $12 per bushel, compared with $10.40 in 2025-26. The soybean meal price is also up $30 at $340 per short ton, and the soybean oil prices was unchanged at 70 cents per pound. Cottonseed The 2026-27 U.S. cotton forecasts show lower production, consumption, and beginning and ending stocks compared to last month. All cotton production is forecast at 13.2 million 480-pound bales, down 3% from the previous forecast and down 5% from 2025. Based on conditions as of Sept. 1, yields are expected to average 776 pounds per harvested acre, down 22 pounds from the previous forecast and down 76 pounds from 2025. Upland cotton production is forecast at 12.8 million 480-pound bales, down 3% from the previous forecast and down 5% from 2025. Pima cotton production is forecast at 392,000 bales, down 2% from the previous month and down 3% from 2025. The 2025-26 season-average farm price forecast is at 62 cents per pound reflecting year-end trade data from census and other sources. The projected season-average price for 2026-27 is raised 2 cents to 78 cents per pound. Alfalfa and other hay The latest USDA Ag Prices report indicated dairy-quality alfalfa hay prices averaged $242 per ton in July, while alfalfa hay prices averaged $203 per ton, and prices for other hay averaged $143 per ton.
