She writ... “Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand. Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness; And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace; Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked. And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God.” (Ephesians 6:11-17 KJV). Paul’s letter is good advice for our day. We battle the same powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. We need the full armor, especially the shield of faith. Faith is things hoped for and not seen. Faith is the power that makes miracles happen. There are things we can make happen by our own efforts, coupled with faith. For example, we have the faith that if we plant a garden and take care of it properly, it will grow and produce what we planted. We have faith that if we exercise, we will change our body’s shape. But there are things we cannot carry out without the power of God. Faith is needed to heal the sick, ward off destructive powers and to change the outcome of a tragedy. Jesus calmed the waves of the sea. Peter walked on water and 5,000 people were fed with five loaves of bread and two fish. Jesus’ miracles were brought about by causative faith. His divine power caused them to happen. We can only tap into those kinds of miracles by building a relationship with Him and using His power to accomplish them by His will. If we have a relationship with Him, we will know that we do not want anything He does not want for us. He will do only what is best for us. Advertisement It’s like the little boy who prayed for lemonade rain from the sky. He had faith and was sad when his prayers were not answered. His wise mother explained everything would die if lemonade rained from the sky. Sometimes the Lord has to say no, even when we have faith. Faith is a belief, an attitude of the mind. What happens in our mind is much more important than the circumstances surrounding our desires. What we believe is possible or impossible happens in our minds. Our perception has everything to do with what we believe. For example, if you see a garden hose in the garden that looks like a snake, you will have the same emotional reaction as if it were a real snake. Your heart will race as you catch your breath. When you realize that it is really a garden hose, you will smile and even laugh at your fears. We create our own thoughts and perceptions by what we think about. What we think of Christ is vital. We must find a true picture of His power and attributes before we can exercise faith in Him. He must become real – not just a picture on a cross somewhere far away – a person who can hear and communicate. The scriptures say, “For as he thinketh in his heart, so is he” (Proverbs 23:7 KJV). In other words, we are the sum total of what we think about. Our mind is like a field of fertile ground waiting to be planted. We can choose to plant whatever we want, and our mind will produce the fruit. If we can control what is planted and nurtured in our minds, we can start to use the power of faith. Self-talk is the key to unlock the power of faith or is the lock that holds the door closed. It is easy to allow random thoughts to circle like an old phonograph record in our minds. We simply allow our mind to play one meaningless song after another. We see images and feel emotions about the things we think about. Often, we do not consider whether our thoughts are grounded in truth or falsehoods. We just let them play, thinking we are a prisoner to our own thoughts. What do we think when we think about Christ? Do we dwell on the many ways He has spoken to us? Or, do we say He will never answer us? That self-talk makes a difference. God has given us the power of choice. We can stop and start the self-talk playing in our minds. We can create our words and emotions. If we truly desire to be blessed to see miracles and Jesus in our lives, we must control our thoughts and our actions. We cannot send out negative thoughts and expect positive results. The Lord says, “Cast thy bread upon the waters: for thou shalt find it after many days” (Ecclesiastes 11:1 KJV). That is the way it is with negative and positive thoughts. If we plant negative thoughts in our minds, negative experiences will grow and flourish. If we plant positive, faith-centered thoughts, we will reap the results. The scriptures tell us, “A double minded man is unstable in all his ways” (James 1:8 KJV). We cannot entertain two opposing viewpoints at the same time. We cannot outwardly say we believe and inwardly say it is not possible. Stronger thoughts will always rule out. The stronger thought shapes our actions. We will act on what we truly believe. Having an eye single to the glory of God has a deeper meaning. If we profess to love God but go about destroying His work by treating our fellow beings with contempt, we are hypocrites and "the truth is not in us" (1 John 2:4 KJV). We cannot serve two masters. “No man can serve two masters: for either he will hate the one and love the other; or else he will hold to the one, and despise the other” (Matthew 6:24 KJV). We are, by nature, compelled to choose sides. We must make a decision, or decisions are made for us. Often, we do not make conscious decisions. We simply let our thoughts run wild. We live from day to day, allowing our lives to march toward the grave without taking any thought of how we shape our destiny. We watch movies that plant thoughts of anger and revenge in our hearts. We listen to music that excites our emotions about someone else’s love affairs. We listen to the gossip about our neighbor and believe the worst. If we look upward, it is usually in a time of crisis, and when the crisis is over, we continue without a second thought. Faith is conscious decision coupled with action. " Faith without works is dead " (James 2:26 KJV). The work we must do is decide. We must decide whose side we are on and make a firm decision to stay there. We must decide what we want and act as though we expect to receive the desire of our hearts. We must choose what self-talk we will allow. Fear and faith are not compatible. When we focus on the reasons why something is impossible, we will find evidence that we are correct. If we focus on the reasons why something is possible, all kinds of possibilities emerge. If we want to have a miracle performed in our lives, we must expect that it will happen. If we are sick and want to be healed, we must avoid thinking about pain and suffering. Instead, we must express gratitude for every evidence of healing. We must visualize with a white-hot focus our body in health and vitality, yet be willing to say, “Thy will be done because, Jesus, my friend, you know what is best for me.” If you are looking for a job, ask God for assistance and believe that He will help. We cannot sit in our overstuffed chairs and expect God to do the work. We must keep looking and be willing to take whatever God gives us until we can find exactly what we want. Our thoughts must ignore all the reasons of can’t and entertain only the thoughts of can. That is the power of faith and the shield we need in these troubled times.
