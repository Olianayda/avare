Home &raquo; Progressive Dairy &raquo; 2026 Farm Bill advances to Senate floor Dairy Industry Issues 2026 Farm Bill advances to Senate floor Kimmi Devaney September 17, 2026 During their Sept. 16 markup session, the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition and Forestry voted 12-11 along party lines to advance the farm bill out of committee with no amendments and no changes. After a failed vote before the August recess, the Agricultural Act of 2026 now moves to the Senate floor for consideration, which is especially timely since the current farm bill extension will expire on Sept. 30. Email Kimmi Devaney Related U.S. Senate committee’s Farm Bill heads to Senate floor 2026 Farm Bill passes the House, moves to the Senate Farm Workforce Modernization Act advances to Senate “With a farm bill already through the House, we’re hopeful that both chambers can now work together on full congressional passage in 2026 that gives dairy farmers the certainty they deserve,” National Milk Producers Federation (NMPF) President and CEO Gregg Doud said in a statement. This passage out of committee builds on momentum earlier this spring by the House of Representatives and the House Committee on Agriculture when they passed the Farm, Food and National Security Act of 2026 . In separate statements, House Committee on Agriculture Chairman Glenn “GT” Thompson (R-Pennsylvania) and U.S. Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition and Forestry Chairman John Boozman (R-Arkansas) shared their commitment to getting the farm bill across the finish line. Amid much uncertainty currently facing America’s farmers, many agriculture organizations are hopeful the passage that a new five-year farm bill will bring some much-needed relief to farm operations. Advertisement “There are real wins for family farmers and ranchers in this farm bill,” National Farmers Union (NFU) President Rob Larew said in a statement. “But we need to hold two truths at once: This farm bill delivers some real improvements, and it will not stop the crisis farmers and ranchers are living through right now. Family farmers are caught between rising input costs, concentrated corporate power and a farm safety net that no longer reflects the reality of farming today. American families are struggling with high prices and lack of robust nutrition programs. Incremental fixes won’t reverse that. We need bigger, bolder reform of farm policy. Without it, farms will keep going out of business, and rural communities and American families will pay the price.” Among those wins, Larew says the country-of-origin labeling (COOL) will help farmers and consumers alike while year-round E15 will open new markets for family farmers nationwide. The U.S. Dairy Export Council (USDEC) adds that the bill takes important steps to strengthen U.S. competitiveness abroad. “[It improves our competitiveness] by advancing efforts to protect the rights of U.S. exporters to continue using common cheese names like ‘Parmesan’ in international markets,” USDEC President and CEO Krysta Harden said in a statement. “The bill also formally moves to USDA the Food for Peace program under which global food aid packages that incorporate U.S. dairy products are administered and redesignates funding to USDA’s proven agricultural export promotion programs, including the Market Access Program. These provisions will help our dairy exporters compete on a more level playing field and expand opportunities in growing overseas markets.” The Consortium for Common Food Names (CCFN) reiterates the importance of protecting the rights to use common names like Parmesan and appreciates the inclusion of the SAFETY Act (Safeguarding American Food and Export Trade Yields Act) in the legislation, which is designed to protect U.S. producers’ rights to use common names in international trade markets. The International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) joins their industry colleagues in commending those involved in passing this bill, which includes nutrition programs important to the dairy industry such as the Dairy Nutrition Incentive Program. “America’s dairy industry, farmers and families need the certainty of a new five-year farm bill, and [this] action brings us meaningfully closer to that goal,” IDFA President and CEO Michael Dykes said in a statement.
