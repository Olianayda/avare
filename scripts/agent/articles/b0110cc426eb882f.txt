Corey Geiger was supposed to be judging Ayrshire heifers. It was 1990, the Manitowoc County 4-H team had won the Wisconsin contest, and the kid who was about to start his senior year of high school walked into World Dairy Expo's ring on the colored shavings and forgot about the heifers. He spent the class looking at the decorations instead. He placed it badly, by his own account, and it never bothered him, because of what he told himself standing there: "I want to be part of the dairy industry the rest of my life." He said as much decades later to Peggy Coffeen on the Up Level Dairy podcast . Listen closely, and you can hear that the kid in the ring is still running the show. Robert Chicoine's version of that moment came decades earlier, and he couldn't even read it. Born on a Quebec dairy farm, he got his first Holstein Journal from a relative who kept purebred cattle. It was in English, and he didn't know any English at all. So he asked his father to tell him the names of the animals in the pictures, and he remembered them. "I don't know why," he told Coffeen in an interview published August 25 . By his account, he'd seen every issue since.
Same ring, same colored shavings, 28 years later: Corey Geiger dances with his wife, Krista, during World Dairy Expo's 2018 Supreme Champion show. This time, he wasn't looking at the decorations.
Neither man had any idea what those moments would turn into. This September, World Dairy Expo is naming Geiger its Industry Person of the Year, Oakfield Corners Dairy its Dairy Producers of the Year , and Chicoine, the boy who memorized cow names he couldn't read, its International Person of the Year.
Ask Geiger who got him here and the list runs long. Bill Borgwardt of Sunnyside Farms watched a teenager read his placings off a clipboard and told him to put it down: "Corey, you know cows. You can tell a good story… tell me a story about those four cows." Geiger was "nervous as heck," and he's been telling stories ever since. Then came the Hoard's Dairyman crew: Bill Knox, Gene Meyer, Ewing Row and Steve Larson. By Geiger's count, they had 120 years of editorial experience between them when he walked in. "And I had one day."
Four editors, one chair
Here's what a Bullvine check of Expo's records turned up. Knox, Meyer, Row and Larson each held the managing editor's chair at Hoard's, according to the magazine's own chronology . Geiger later held it for 14 of his nearly 30 years there, per Expo's citation . Three of those four mentors are already on Expo's Industry Person of the Year list , the same award Geiger receives this month.
That's a 56-year span of award dates, from Knox to Geiger. It doesn't prove mentorship causes awards, and it isn't a record of continuous teaching. It does make "somebody handed this to me first" a lot more concrete than the usual acceptance-speech line.
Six names, one award
Oakfield's honor makes the same point in a different way: it names six people, not one . Jonathan and Alicia Lamb, Matthew and Kendra Lamb, Jim Veazey and Janette Veazey-Post. A championship photo fits one cow and one halter, and running 10,000-plus cows across two states takes a lot more hands than that. Add Chicoine, who made sure his board got credit for the idea behind Boviteq, and it's three for three: nobody here got here alone, and nobody's keeping the credit to themselves.
