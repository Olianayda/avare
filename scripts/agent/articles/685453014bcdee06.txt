Home &raquo; Blogs &raquo; Progressive Dairy The Courage to Evolve &raquo; The Courage to Evolve: Valuing the people around us The Courage to Evolve: Valuing the people around us Hank Wagner August 25, 2026 A number of years ago, I was feeding cows on a quiet Sunday morning. Some of my best thinking has happened while feeding or milking cows. There is something about the steady rhythm of farm work that allows the mind to wander and the heart to reflect in ways that rarely happen during the busyness of everyday life. Email Hank Wagner That particular morning, my thoughts settled on a man from our neighborhood named Herb Kanzenbach. When I was growing up, Herb harvested the oats on our farm with two small combines. He also pulled a homemade grain cart that I loved riding in as a young boy. As I continued feeding cows, memories of Herb began flooding back. His combines were old and seemed to break down regularly, yet I never once remember hearing him complain, lose his temper or use foul language. No matter what happened, Herb remained calm, patient and kind. As one memory followed another, I noticed they all carried the same theme. Herb had quietly demonstrated the kind of character I admired, even though I did not fully recognize it at the time. It suddenly became clear that some of the patience I had developed as a husband and father had been shaped simply by watching him respond to difficult situations. By the time I finished feeding cows nearly three hours later, I had made a decision. I needed to go to Herb's house that very day and tell him how much he had impacted my life. Advertisement When I pulled into his driveway, I noticed his daughter sitting outside in a lawn chair. I parked the truck and walked over to ask if Herb was home. She paused before answering, and I could immediately see sadness in her eyes. She told me Herb was inside. I asked if I could visit him. She said I could but gently explained that he might not be able to hear me or respond. Herb was in the final hours of his life. I had not seen him for several years. When I walked into his room, I barely recognized him. He looked frail and weak, with his eyes closed as he rested quietly. I sat beside him and began telling him about that morning. I shared how memory after memory had reminded me of the influence he had on my life. I apologized for never realizing until then just how much he had taught me without ever intending to. I thanked him for the example he had set and explained that his patience had helped make me a better husband and father. I also told him that his influence had reached far beyond me because it had touched my family as well. Herb never spoke a single word. But I know he heard me. As I finished speaking, he slowly opened his eyes, and tears rolled down his cheeks. I held his hand for a while, gave him a hug, kissed him on the forehead and quietly left. I have never forgotten that day. It taught me that opportunities pass by us every single day, and many of those opportunities come disguised as people. I also believe people are strategically placed in our lives to help shape who we become. Sometimes they teach us through words. More often, they teach us through the quiet example of how they live. I believe every one of us has a Herb somewhere in our story. We have all been influenced by people who may never fully realize the impact they had on our lives. Their kindness, integrity, patience, generosity or encouragement became part of who we are. Take a few moments to think about the people who have been your Herbs. Then consider reaching out to them. Write a handwritten note. Take them to lunch. Give them a meaningful gift. Tell them specifically how they changed your life. If your children are old enough, bring them along so they can witness the importance of honoring people who have invested in others. Years ago, when our children were in school, they were blessed to have a husband and wife who both served as exceptional teachers. Their influence on our children went far beyond academics. We decided to invite them to dinner at a nice restaurant with our children present so we could intentionally honor them for the difference they had made. It became a memorable evening for everyone involved. Those teachers remain dear friends today, and they continue to influence our family. Even more rewarding has been watching our own children grow into adults who now intentionally honor the people who have positively impacted their lives. Our world has countless ways to communicate, yet it often does a poor job of making people feel genuinely valued. That responsibility belongs to each of us. Most people who shape our lives never do it for recognition. They simply live with character, humility and kindness, allowing those qualities to overflow into the lives of others. Perhaps the greatest gift we can give them is making sure they know their life truly mattered.
