Barn curtains: Winter preparation checklist Matt Armstrong September 15, 2026 Before winter arrives, take time to inspect and maintain your barn curtain system. Addressing small issues now can help improve ventilation, protect animal comfort and avoid unexpected breakdowns during the coldest months of the year. As we get closer to winter, most dairy producers are busy making sure feed is in place, equipment is serviced and bedding supplies are ready. One thing that can sometimes get pushed down the list is checking barn curtains. It may not seem like a big job, but a little time spent on curtain maintenance before cold weather arrives can save a lot of headaches later. They help control airflow, reduce drafts and allow fresh air into the barn. When curtains aren't working properly, cows and calves can end up dealing with too much cold air, excess moisture or poor air quality. The good news is that most curtain issues can be found and fixed before winter if you take the time to inspect the system. Start with a walk around the barn One of the easiest things you can do is simply walk around the barn and look closely at all the curtains. Check both the inside and outside of the barn if possible. Look for tears or holes in the curtain material, loose hardware, damaged cables, bent brackets and areas where the curtain isn't sealing properly. Advertisement Even small holes can become larger problems once winter winds start blowing. If you find damage, it is usually easier and cheaper to repair it now than wait until January when it's -20°C and snowing sideways. Make sure everything works Many producers don't discover there’s a problem until the curtain needs to move during a cold snap. Open and close each curtain completely and watch how it operates. Does it roll up evenly? Does it close tightly? Is the motor making unusual noises? Are there any spots where it sticks or binds? If you have automatic controls, make sure the controller, sensors and motors are all working properly. A curtain that won't close during a winter storm can create a lot of problems in a hurry. Check for drafts One thing that often gets overlooked is air leaks around the ends of the curtain or where sections overlap. During the winter, you want fresh air entering the barn where you expect it to. Random gaps and openings can create drafts right where cows are lying down. Take a look at curtain ends, overlap areas, corners and door openings. If you can see daylight coming through where it shouldn't, chances are cold air will find its way in too. Don’t forget the hardware Most people pay attention to the curtain itself, but the hardware is just as important. Check cables, pulleys, bearings and gearboxes for wear. Look for rust, frayed cables or loose bolts. Replacing a worn cable in the fall is a lot easier than trying to fix one that breaks in the middle of a snowstorm. Lubricate moving parts if recommended by the manufacturer and tighten anything that has worked loose over the year. Thinking about new curtains? If your curtain system is getting older or you are building a new barn, winter preparation season can be a good time to evaluate whether your current setup is still meeting your needs. Not all curtain systems are the same. When looking at new curtains, there are a few things worth considering.
Quality of the material.  Cheaper curtain materials may save money upfront, but they often don't last as long. A good curtain should stand up to sunlight, wind and cold temperatures without becoming brittle or tearing easily.
Ease of operation.  Whether the system is manual or automated, it should operate smoothly and reliably. Nobody wants to spend time fighting with a curtain system every time the weather changes.
Automation options.  Many newer barns are using automated curtain systems that adjust based on temperature and weather conditions. These systems can help maintain a more consistent barn environment and reduce the amount of daily adjustment required by employees or family members.
Don’t close everything up One common mistake during winter is trying to keep the barn too warm by closing everything up tight. Cows still need fresh air, even when it is cold outside. When ventilation is reduced too much, moisture starts to build up inside the barn. That can lead to wet bedding, condensation and poor air quality. If you're seeing water on windows, steel beams or ceilings, it is usually a sign that the barn needs more ventilation. The goal isn't necessarily to keep cows warm. Healthy dairy cows actually handle cold temperatures quite well. The goal is to provide fresh air while preventing direct drafts. Pay special attention to calf barns Calves can be more sensitive to drafts than mature cows. When checking curtains in calf facilities, spend some time at calf level. Conditions often feel different where the calves are housed compared to where people are standing. A properly adjusted curtain system should help keep calves dry and comfortable while still providing enough fresh air to maintain good air quality. Make curtain maintenance part of your routine Like most equipment around the farm, curtain systems last longer when they receive regular attention. A simple annual inspection can help identify problems before they become expensive repairs. Most issues don't happen overnight. They usually start small and gradually get worse over time. By spending a few hours checking curtains, repairing damage and making sure everything is operating properly before winter arrives, producers can avoid many of the problems that show up during the coldest months of the year. Final thoughts Barn curtains may not be the most exciting piece of equipment on the farm, but they play a big role in maintaining a healthy barn environment throughout the winter. Taking the time to inspect, repair and maintain curtains before cold weather arrives can help improve cow comfort, maintain better air quality and prevent unexpected breakdowns when conditions are at their worst. A little preventative maintenance in the fall can go a long way toward making winter barn management easier for both cows and producers. Curtain preparation checklist Open and close each curtain completely and watch how it operates.
Does it roll up evenly?
Does it close tightly?
Is the motor making unusual noises?
Are there any spots where it sticks or binds?
Areas to inspect:
Tears or holes in the curtain material
Loose hardware
Damaged cables
Bent brackets
Areas where the curtain isn't sealing properly
Cables, pulleys, bearings and gearboxes
Lubrication of moving parts
Calf barn drafts (observe the drafts at calf level)
