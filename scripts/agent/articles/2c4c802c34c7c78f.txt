Markets improve for an October advanced Class I base price of $18.88 per cwt After months of receding Class I base prices, the tides turned in October as the advanced Class I base price improved for the first time since June. Announced Sept. 23, the Federal Milk Marketing Order (FMMO) advanced Class I base price came in at $18.88 per hundredweight (cwt) in October. That’s a price improvement of $1.84 per cwt from the previous month and 84 cents per cwt above the advanced Class I base price of October 2025. Class I zone differentials are added to the base price principal pricing points to determine the actual Class I price in each FMMO. With those additions, October’s Class I base price should average $23 per cwt across all orders. The highest price should be reported in the Florida FMMO at $25.68 per cwt, and the lowest price should be noticed in the Arizona FMMO at $21.48 per cwt. The October Class I base skim milk price was $14.12 per cwt, up from September’s $12.16 per cwt. The spread in the monthly advanced Class III skim milk pricing factor ($11.18 per cwt) and the advanced Class IV skim milk pricing factor ($14.12 per cwt) widened to $2.94 as Class IV was once again the higher-of for the Class I mover in the milk pricing formula. At this margin, the current milk pricing formula results in the higher base price as has been realized in most months this year. Advertisement The advanced butterfat pricing factor was down 2 cents from September for a price of $1.50 per pound in October. The advanced Class I base price and pooling percentages will impact regional FMMO uniform milk prices. September regional prices will be announced Oct. 9-14, and the October regional prices will be announced a month later, Nov. 9-11. Dairy Management Inc. board selects Corey Scott as new president and CEO Following a comprehensive national search, the Dairy Management Inc. (DMI) board of directors announced the appointment of Corey Scott as president and chief executive officer. Scott will become the third president and CEO in DMI’s history, succeeding Barbara O’Brien, who will retire following the 2026 joint annual meeting of the National Dairy Promotion and Research Board, National Milk Producers Federation and United Dairy Industry Association, Oct. 19-21. The appointment follows a months-long succession process conducted by the DMI board of directors to identify the leader best positioned to lead strategies designed to grow dairy sales, strengthen consumer trust and deliver value to the dairy farmers and importers who fund the checkoff. “We are so excited for the next phase of DMI where Corey will focus on our program areas, strengthen our relationships and bring efficiencies to the incredible work that Barb O’Brien advanced during the past five years,” said Marilyn Hershey, a Pennsylvania dairy farmer and chair of the DMI board of directors. “Corey will bring a positive and effective energy to the federation and will continue the amazing work that is implemented for dairy farmers and importers across the country. The search committee unanimously brought her forward, knowing her leadership skills, industry knowledge and humble service will carry us to the next level and secure strong opportunities for the future of dairy.” As president and CEO, Scott will oversee more than 150 DMI and National Dairy Council employees on behalf of more than 23,000 dairy farmers and dairy importers. Scott also will serve as president and CEO of the checkoff-founded Innovation Center for U.S. Dairy. Scott comes to DMI after serving as president and CEO of Midwest Dairy, where she led the dairy checkoff across a 10-state region representing 41.8 million consumers and more than 3,600 dairy farm families. Before joining Midwest Dairy in 2024, Scott served as the vice president of sales and marketing for Athian and spent more than 15 years in leadership positions with Land O’Lakes. Zoetis to debut new location and attendee experience at World Dairy Expo Zoetis invites attendees to its new location and experience at the 2026 World Dairy Expo. This year, Zoetis is moving from the Exhibition Hall to the Coliseum Lower Level. Designed as a welcoming place for dairy producers, veterinarians, industry partners and experts from across the dairy industry, the new space will give attendees multiple ways to connect, engage and experience what’s moving dairy forward. Throughout the week, attendees will be able to hear from visionary dairy leaders and industry experts during the Dairy On stage talks, gather over Wisconsin-inspired favorites at the Milky Whey Lounge, add their farm to the Born of the Bond Wall and explore milestones that have shaped dairy innovation on their way to a railside view of the Coliseum show ring. “World Dairy Expo has always been about bringing the dairy community together, and we wanted the new Zoetis space to reflect that spirit,” said Leah Beyer, head of marketing communications, U.S. livestock, Zoetis. “Our new World Dairy Expo space is built around what makes this industry strong, and those are relationships, shared ideas and a drive to keep moving dairy forward.” Dairy On stage talks

McCarty Family Farms, MVP Dairy and Cargill debut new interactive dairy nutrition exhibits McCarty Family Farms has opened a new interactive dairy nutrition exhibit at the Judy McCarty Dairy Learning Center in Rexford, Kansas, with support from the Cargill Cares Council. A companion exhibit is also open at the MVP Dairy Learning Center in Celina, Ohio. Together, the exhibits give students, families and community members a hands-on way to learn how animal nutrition supports cow health, dairy farm performance and milk quality. The two learning centers have welcomed more than 100,000 in-person and virtual visitors since opening from all 50 states and more than 75 countries. “For our family, helping people better understand where their food comes from and how we care for our animals is an important part of the future of dairy,” said Ken McCarty, co-owner of McCarty Family Farms and MVP Dairy. “Our relationship with Cargill goes well beyond providing nutrition for our cows. They have been a trusted partner who understands our farm, our goals and the importance nutrition plays in the health and care of our herd.” “Strong dairy farms start with healthy animals,” said Phil Graham, managing director of Cargill Animal Nutrition & Health North America. “We are proud to work alongside the McCarty and VanTilburg families to bring together their dairy expertise with Cargill’s nutrition capabilities and innovation. Supporting these learning centers is one way we can help connect communities with agriculture and inspire the people who will shape what comes next.” Visitors to the Kansas and Ohio learning centers can also tour the dairy farms, view the cows being milked and learn about cow care alongside environmental stewardship.  Compeer Financial awards agricultural education grants to 69 middle, high schools Nearly 70 middle and high schools received $446,318 in grants from Compeer Financial to support agricultural education programs. The Next A.C.R.E. grant, part of the Compeer Giving program, awarded up to $7,500 to each recipient school, with 21 Illinois, 21 Minnesota and 27 Wisconsin schools receiving a grant. Funds are used for equipment and resources that advance hands-on learning and support renovations, improvements and building projects for non-traditional classrooms, such as greenhouses, shops and livestock barns. “With the Next A.C.R.E. grant, Compeer Financial intends to help ag education programs keep pace with today’s rapidly evolving agricultural landscape and give students the opportunity to elevate their agricultural knowledge and skills,” said Karen Schieler, manager of Compeer Giving at Compeer Financial. “Advancements in technology across ag sectors make it important to provide middle and high school students with equipment and resources that accurately represent the opportunities available within an industry that feeds, fuels and clothes us all. We hope this funding inspires students to pursue a career in agriculture where they can make a lasting impact on their local communities and the world.” To qualify for a Next A.C.R.E. grant, applicants must offer agriculture education classes at a middle or high school within Compeer Financial’s 144-county territory. Compeer Financial will again offer the grant in 2027. Bobcat Company, employees give back through global volunteerism Bobcat Company and its employees recently came together to support communities through Doosan Bobcat Community Days and the company’s annual Fall Giving Campaign – contributing both hands-on volunteer support and charitable donations that made a meaningful impact in the communities where employees live and work. As part of Doosan Bobcat Community Days, employees across North America and other global locations participated in volunteer projects supporting local nonprofits and community organizations. These efforts supported initiatives focused on education, environmental stewardship and community development. In many cases, Bobcat equipment was used to help complete projects more efficiently, reinforcing the company’s ability to make a tangible difference in the communities it serves. More than 200 employees contributed 2,000 volunteer hours in support of community projects across North America. Key initiatives included clearing and creating nature trails, picking up litter, renovating community campsites and planting trees. In addition to volunteering during Doosan Bobcat Community Days, employees also supported their communities through charitable giving during the company’s annual Fall Giving campaign. Bobcat employees in the North America region supported nonprofit organizations of their choice, with Bobcat matching donations to amplify the impact of each contribution. In total, Bobcat and its employees donated more than $100,000 to 80 nonprofit organizations across North America. Bobcat matched donations dollar-for-dollar – up to $3,000 per employee – expanding the overall impact of employee contributions. Throughout the year, Bobcat partners with organizations that advance its community engagement priorities, including STEM education, workforce development and disaster relief. As part of this commitment, the company recently announced a $1 million multiyear investment in the Fargo-Moorhead Science Museum to help develop hands-on STEM experiences that inspire future generations. Bobcat also expanded its partnership with The Salvation Army through an additional $465,000 equipment donation – bringing total contributions to more than $1.35 million since 2024.
