Image courtesy of VAS. Connected dairy data has been decades in the making Jennifer Stamey and Mark Moran September 14, 2026 Herd management software helped move dairy records from paper to digital. Now, it is helping producers prepare for the next generation of connected decision-making. When you picture your dairy in five, 10 or even 20 years from now, what would make your day-to-day decisions easier? Rather than asking teams to interpret each stream of information separately, the system could surface what matters most – which cows need attention, which tasks are most urgent and where the team should focus first. While that vision may seem futuristic, its foundation has been built through decades of progress. Today, connected data is helping farms organize the day before boots hit the ground. The next evolution will unlock even more value from the data they already have. Built on a familiar foundation Herd management software was one of the industry’s original connected data systems, with some of the first programs introduced more than 40 years ago. The shift from paper records to digital systems gave producers a more practical way to capture, analyze and act on herd information. Instead of stacks of reports in the farm office, teams could create lists, set protocols and bring information together in a way that previously required significant time and effort. While paper records showed where a herd had been, digital records made it easier to look ahead. Producers could see herd trends faster and use that information to guide decisions around breeding, feeding, treatment, movement and culling. Advertisement Integrating Dairy Herd Improvement Association (DHIA) records helped bring cow-level information into a single source of truth, creating the framework for the visibility, connectivity and powerful analysis today’s dairies rely on. Herd records at the center Trusted herd records are central as dairies add more technology. Collars, tags, sort gates, parlor systems, genomic tools, feed systems and other technologies can all add value, but only if the data can connect in a way that supports better decisions. Today’s dairy data is growing in scale and scope. There are more cows, more devices, more data sources and more types of information flowing into the operation. While the amount of information continues to grow, so does the opportunity to connect it all into a clearer picture of the operation. When those sources work together instead of operating individually, the resulting insights become richer, faster and more useful for the people managing the dairy. Connected data at work today While connected data can sound like a big-picture concept, many dairies are already putting it to work in everyday decisions and workflows. Often, the herd management system serves as the hub, bringing information from different technologies together and triggering the next action. For example:
An activity monitoring collar sends a high-activity alert to the herd management system, which automatically adds the cow to a breeding list.
The herd management system communicates with a sort gate to automatically separate cows needing attention, instead of employees walking pens to search for them.
A reader identifies a cow and the herd management system displays the treatments she needs on a mobile device.
Health and behavior data flow into the herd management system, which alerts employees to cows that may need intervention before it becomes more severe.
Some of the most meaningful connected-data examples are simple, but the flexibility they create for dairy teams is invaluable. Real-time information and alerts delivered to a phone can help you stay connected whether you are at home, at a child’s sports game, at an off-farm meeting or simply away from the barn or office. That connectivity can provide peace of mind, give you time back and support the lifestyle many family farms are working to preserve. What connected systems help remove One of the greatest benefits of having connected systems is that they help create consistency across people, processes and data. When information is split between multiple systems, notebooks or people’s memories, it creates opportunities for duplicate entries, missed tasks and communication gaps. A trusted source of truth helps protect the operation by preventing too much knowledge from being tied to a single person. Preparing for the future starts with strengthening the quality and connection of data today. That does not mean every dairy needs every new tool. It means looking for places where paper, duplicate entry or disconnected workflows are slowing the team down or creating risk. When technology can automate recordkeeping, data is often timelier and more accurate. For example, scanning a cow and automatically recording the completed task cowside removes opportunities for numbers to be transposed, notes to be lost or data entry to be delayed. Helping teams focus where it matters The opportunity is to let connected systems handle more of the computational heavy lifting so producers and herd teams can focus their experience, creativity and cow knowledge where they create the most value. That support can extend across the team. As farms grow, more employees enter the agriculture workforce without a dairy background. Connected data can help make tasks clearer, surface priorities and provide more context for the work they are asked to do. As the connected dairy continues to evolve, the goal is not technology for technology’s sake. It is helping producers run their operation the way they want, with better visibility, stronger workflows and more confidence in their decisions.   Ultimately, better connectivity should provide more than better data. It should give you more freedom and peace of mind in how you manage your dairy. Prepare for the dairy you want to manage Preparing for that desired future starts with choosing systems that fit your dairy’s current needs and long-term goals. Your dairy is unique, from your management style to your plans for growth, which is why it’s important to invest in solutions that support where you are today while giving you room to adapt. A strong platform should provide that flexibility as your dairy evolves. The connected dairy of the future will be more automated, more predictive and more real-time than ever. But the goal of that technology will remain familiar: help you better understand your cows, support their well-being, empower your teams and make better decisions using information you trust. Signs your dairy may be ready for better connectivity
Employees enter the same information into multiple systems.
Teams still rely heavily on paper lists or notebooks.
Managers spend time searching for cows or confirming completed tasks.
Important knowledge is concentrated with one employee.
Systems collect useful data but do not share it.
Information reaches the team too late to guide action.
