“A meta-analysis and meta-regression to compare the production performance, feed efficiency and milk nitrogen efficiency in Jersey versus Holstein cows.” Journal of Dairy Science  Vol. 109 No. 7, 2026 This article, written by researchers from Canada, Brazil and the U.S., describes the results of a meta-analysis and meta-regression to compare dry matter intake (DMI), production performance, feed efficiency (FE) and milk nitrogen efficiency (MNE; milk nitrogen yield/nitrogen intake) of Holstein and Jersey cows. The central hypothesis was that feed efficiency as energy-corrected milk would be greater in Jerseys than Holsteins, with MNE not affected by breed. The authors used only experiments in which purebred Holsteins and Jerseys enrolled in the same study, under similar experimental conditions, were used to build the datasets (n = 30 peer-reviewed articles). The studies had a total of 1,478 cows (806 Holsteins and 673 Jerseys). The authors ran a meta-regression to explore the effect of continuous (year of publication, days in milk and dietary concentrations of NDF, CP and forage) and categorical (forage type: corn silage, alfalfa/grass-clover mix and other forage sources [“others”]) co-variates on the weighed raw mean of different variables.
