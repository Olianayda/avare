Input sought on proposed Waters of the U.S. definition The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the U.S. Department of the Army announced a Supplemental Notice of Proposed Rulemaking to gather public input on additional regulatory language for defining “waters of the United States” (WOTUS) to be considered alongside the 2025 proposed rule. This action reflects the agencies’ commitment to transparency and to developing a durable, final WOTUS definition that adheres to the Supreme Court’s direction in Sackett v. EPA , cuts bureaucratic red tape and protects water quality while recognizing that states and tribes are best positioned to manage their own water resources. “Today’s action reflects our continued commitment to transparency and public input, ensuring we have fully considered a wide range of potential policy options. We look forward to public feedback on this supplemental notice, which will help strengthen the real-world expertise behind the final rule,” said EPA Administrator Lee Zeldin. Once the supplemental proposal is published in the Federal Register, the agencies will accept public comments for 30 days. Advertisement Bovine veterinarians tasked with ‘Developing Practices of Excellence’ at the 2026 AABP Annual Conference The American Association of Bovine Practitioners’ (AABP) 2026 59th Annual Conference, Aug. 27-29 in Minneapolis, Minnesota, assisted bovine veterinarians in “Developing Practices of Excellence.” Beef and dairy veterinarians, students and veterinary technicians gathered for this conference for cutting-edge continuing education. Keynote speaker Dr. Eric Rooker presented, “Flourishing veterinary medicine: A game of dollars and sense.” He suggested to find some time as practice for owners to let others, such as new associates, develop some competency of their own, and gave examples on how to do that. Following Rooker’s presentation, the 2026 AABP vice president candidates, Dr. Becky Funk and Dr. John Groves, were introduced to attendees. All AABP members who were not able to attend are encouraged to take advantage of the recorded sessions continuing education (CE) opportunity through Kansas State University’s Beef Cattle Institute, where all CE sessions are available. The 2026 conference gave in-person attendees up to 26.25 hours of continuing education (plus more for preconference seminars) on beef, dairy, clinical stills, retention, hot topics, mixed animal, practice management, student/new graduate topics, practice tips and other sessions, plus preconference seminars and clinical forums. Awards, a scholarship auction and a 5K were also highlights of the conference. Several bovine veterinarians were honored at the annual business meeting and awards luncheon. Dr. Mark E. Fox, Sandusky, Michigan, received the AABP Bovine Practitioner of the Year Award. This award honors a veterinarian in active practice who is active in organized veterinary medicine and has made significant contributions to bovine medicine. Receiving the AABP Award of Excellence was Dr. Richard Wallace, Bonita Springs, Florida, and Dr. Margaret Masterson, Plain City, Ohio, was awarded Merck Animal Health Mentor of the Year. Dr. Kaitlyn Briggs, Sherburne, New York, received the Dr. James A. Jarrett Award for Young Leaders. Nominations open for NMC’s Award of Excellence honor NMC: The Global Milk Quality Organization is now accepting nominations for its 2027 Award of Excellence for Contribution to Mastitis Prevention and Control. This award honors an NMC member who has provided sustained contributions to mastitis prevention, treatment and control through research, extension and/or education, clinical practice or service to dairy producers. The award will not be given posthumously. Nominations close Oct. 31. The award includes a travel stipend to attend the NMC 66th Annual Meeting, Jan. 25-28, in Jacksonville, Florida, where the award will be presented during the awards luncheon on Jan. 28. To nominate an individual, complete the nomination form, which can be found online . In addition to the completed form, submit at least two letters of support. These letters should describe how the nominee has met award criteria, including time in the dairy industry, positions held, major contributions to mastitis prevention and milk quality, research, publications, leadership roles, participation in NMC activities and industry involvement. Also, include the nominee’s CV (curriculum vitae) and/or professional resume. 2027 internship opportunities with Holstein Association USA and Holstein Foundation Holstein Association USA and Holstein Foundation are now accepting applications for two paid summer internships. The digital content and communications intern will gain hands-on experience creating multimedia content while telling the stories of registered Holstein breeders and highlighting the programs and services offered by Holstein Association USA. The Holstein Foundation youth programs intern will gain experience in youth development and event planning, while supporting educational opportunities for young people in the dairy industry. Applications for both internships are due Oct. 31. Interns will play an active role in two major industry events, the 2027 National Holstein Convention and the inaugural Dairy Data Academy, working alongside industry leaders from across the country and building valuable professional connections. Running from mid-May through mid-August 2027, both paid internships are based in Brattleboro, Vermont. Furnished housing in the area is provided for the duration of the internship. For complete position descriptions and to apply, visit the internship website . Castaneda to retire from NMPF in December 2026 National Milk Producers Federation (NMPF) Executive Vice President Jaime Castaneda, one of the U.S. dairy industry’s longest-serving and most influential trade policy leaders, announced his plans to retire from the organization in December 2026 after more than 27 years of service with NMPF and the U.S. Dairy Export Council (USDEC). Castaneda has led international policy efforts for the organizations since joining NMPF and USDEC in 1999, helping transform the U.S. dairy industry into a globally competitive, export-oriented sector while advancing policies that protect and strengthen America’s dairy farmers and manufacturers. In addition, Castaneda played a leadership role for NMPF government relations over the years, leading the construction of today’s policies. “Serving America’s dairy farmers and this great industry has been the honor of a lifetime,” Castaneda said. “I am deeply grateful to the leadership of NMPF and USDEC, to our members and to the countless colleagues, partners and friends I have worked with over the years. Together, we helped build a globally competitive U.S. dairy industry that today is respected around the world.” Castaneda will continue serving as executive director of the Consortium for Common Food Names (CCFN), where he has led international efforts to protect the use of common food and beverage terms against restrictive geographical indication policies. He also plans to continue supporting the dairy industry following his retirement from NMPF by remaining engaged with USDEC, as well as other food and agricultural organizations and companies, while spending more time with family and hiking in the mountains. Among his many accomplishments, Castaneda helped lead the dairy industry’s “Foundation for the Future” initiative, which modernized U.S. dairy pricing policy to improve competitiveness in global markets and served as the basis for today’s Dairy Margin Coverage Program. Global theatrical screening tour announced for World Without Cows The World Without Cows filmmaking team and Studio 410 released the official trailer for the award-winning documentary and announced its fall global theatrical screening tour. It kicks off with global premiere events in Dublin, Sept. 9; London, Sept. 10; Los Angeles, Sept. 16; and New York, Sept. 18; with directors Michelle Michael and Brandon Whitworth and executive producer Dr. Mark Lyons in attendance. Against a backdrop of climate change, food insecurity and rising protein demand, World Without Cows challenges what we think we know about cows, climate, health and the future of our planet. Through a global journey spanning 22 countries and five continents, the film explores the complex relationship between food, sustainability, biodiversity and the people whose lives hang in the balance. Following the premieres, the campaign expands into a theatrical tour of select cities throughout North America beginning this October, including a large-scale journey through America’s heartland, alongside international screening events planned across South America, Europe, Africa and Asia, returning World Without Cows to many of the places where it was filmed. Most stops pair the film with panel discussions, making this theatrical release a town hall, gathering farmers and ranchers, scientists and students, educators, environmental voices, food and agriculture communities, and moviegoers in the same room to watch and talk together. Through a partnership with Gathr, any community can also request a screening and bring the film to its own local theater. With the film available in more than 25 languages, the tour is built to travel. Additional tour cities, international screening events, participating panelists and special screenings will be announced in the coming weeks.
