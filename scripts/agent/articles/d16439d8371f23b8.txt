Peel August 31, 2026 The slow-turning cattle cycle means that cattle and beef prices will continue to be strong. Thus far in 2026, cattle and beef markets continued the strong markets that began in late 2022. All classes of cattle set new record highs in the first half of the year. Summer heat and announced changes in Mexican border trade provoked a sharp futures-led market correction in July before markets resumed in August. Tight cattle supplies and strong beef demand remain the dominant market fundamentals, and the overall trajectory of the industry has not changed. Derrell S. Table 1 summarizes the 2026 inventory compared to one year earlier. The total cattle inventory was essentially unchanged with the beef cow herd down 0.7% and the dairy cow herd up 2.1%. The inventory of replacement heifers increased with beef heifers up 2.7% year over year and dairy heifers up 2.9%. The 2026 calf crop is estimated at 32.5 million head, down 1.5% year over year. The estimated July 1 feeder supply was down 0.6% and a smaller calf crop suggests that feeder supplies will continue to tighten into 2027. Beef production Total beef production was down 5.2% year over year in the first 28 weeks of the year ( Figure 1 ). Sharp decreases in total cattle slaughter were only partially offset by heavier carcass weights. Total fed (steer and heifer) slaughter was down 8.5% from last year with steer slaughter down 6.8% and heifer slaughter down 11.1%. Steer carcass weights are up 34.1 pounds, and heifer carcasses are up 26.3 pounds from one year ago.   Total cow slaughter is down 4.5% year over year, with beef cow slaughter down 14.3% year over year and dairy cow slaughter up 4.8% thus far in 2026.  Advertisement After decreasing 3.6% last year, total beef production is forecast to decrease roughly 4.5% year over year in 2026 and to decrease further in 2027. Beef demand Wholesale and retail beef prices have continued strong this year, driven by resilient consumer preferences for beef and by tighter beef supplies. Choice boxed beef prices have averaged 10% higher than one year ago in the first seven months of the year. Data through June shows that retail all-fresh beef prices are up 14.8% year over year. Retail beef prices have continued to increase relative to pork and broiler prices despite ample supplies of other proteins. Prolonged high gasoline prices are a concern for beef demand but has not affected beef demand measurably thus far. Cattle and beef trade Beef exports and imports have behaved as expected with decreased beef production and higher prices in the U.S. Beef exports are down 15.8% year over year through the first half of the year. Through 2025, beef exports were down 27.2% since the 2022 record export level. In 2026, South Korea is the largest beef export market, followed by Japan, Mexico, Canada, China/Hong Kong and Taiwan. Beef imports have been increasing to moderate declining U.S. beef production. Total beef imports through 2025 are up 58.9% since 2022, the peak in beef production. Beef imports are up another 12% in the first six months of 2026. Major sources of beef imports are Brazil, Australia, Canada, Mexico, New Zealand and Uruguay. North American cattle and beef markets are highly integrated. In addition to bilateral beef trade with Canada and Mexico, trade in live cattle is important as well. In 2025, the U.S. imported 751,563 head of cattle from Canada and exported 541,940 head of cattle to Canada. The U.S. exports mostly feeder cattle to Canada and imports fed cattle, cull cows and bulls and some feeder cattle from Canada. Historically, the U.S. has imported about 1.15 million head of Mexican feeder cattle per year, equal to about 3.2% of the U.S. calf crop. The U.S. typically exports 30,000 to 40,000 head of cattle to Mexico annually. Because of New World screwworm (NWS), the border has been closed completely to imports of Mexican cattle since mid-July 2025 and mostly closed since November 2024. At the time of this writing, the USDA announced a phased reopening of the border, which is set to begin in late August. A relatively small number of cattle are expected initially with slow expansion of the volume for several months. It is unlikely that significant market impacts will be detectable as a result of resumed cattle imports. Dairy sector implications Cattle and beef markets are impacting the dairy sector as never before. High cattle prices since 2023 have provided significant revenue opportunities for dairy operations and are shaping dairy herd management decisions for the first time. Record-high calf prices and the feasibility of beef-on-dairy crossbreeding has led to record-high neonatal dairy crossbred calf prices. This prompted reduced dairy cow culling in 2025 to the lowest level in 17 years and allowed the dairy cow herd to expand to the largest inventory since 1993. Dairy cow slaughter is higher in 2026 as older cows must necessarily be culled. Dairy heifer retention is also higher to maintain dairy herd numbers and productivity. Neonatal calf prices are expected to remain strong along with cull cow prices. The beef market contribution will continue to be important for the dairy sector. What to expect going forward There are signs of a slow turn in the cattle cycle. The beef cow herd may stabilize close to current levels. Limited heifer retention is underway but little, if any, herd growth is possible in 2026 and 2027. Additional heifer retention will further squeeze cattle supplies for one to two years. Cattle prices are expected to continue edging higher through the remainder of this year and into 2027 at least.
