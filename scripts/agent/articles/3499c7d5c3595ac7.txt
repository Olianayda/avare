Home &raquo; Progressive Cattle &raquo; Cattle industry to Trump: Stop the cheap beef imports Cattle Policy Issues Cattle industry to Trump: Stop the cheap beef imports David Cooper August 28, 2026 The administration has pushed policy to open U.S. to cheaper beef trim supply coming from unspecified countries and now wants beef production laws allowing producers to process their own beef. Several prominent cattle producer groups and beef industry leaders rallied this week to voice their concern, if not outrage, at President Donald Trump’s trade announcement opening the U.S. market to cheaper beef imports. David Cooper Managing Editor / Progressive Cattle Email David Cooper Related Senators join to oppose Trump's Argentina beef imports Trump signs order to increase Argentine beef imports Cattlemen reject Trump’s plan for Argentina beef imports The National Cattlemen’s Beef Association and the U.S. Cattlemen’s Association were joined by the Kansas Livestock Association and the American Farm Bureau in statements criticizing Trump for allowing more imports from unspecified countries to lower beef prices. The president has also pushed plans to reduce federal meat inspection and food safety protocols related to beef processing, and authorizing farmers and ranchers to process their own meat. The import opposition rose after Trump announced in a Truth Social post on Aug. 21, a 90-day deal to bring in 300,000 metric tons of “product for ground beef to be imported with no out-of-quota tariff” and that it would be sold “at 25 percent below current market prices.” Trump gave no indication what countries were providing the supply. The post was deflating for several cattle industry experts, as it came just days before the U.S. border was to open to Mexican import cattle at border stations in Arizona and New Mexico, after a year of closure to New World screwworm concerns. Feeders in the U.S. have relied on live cattle imports from Mexico for several years, using them to produce high-quality fed cattle for U.S. consumers. In a statement released Friday, NCBA said, “For generations, cattle producers have invested in building consumer confidence in American beef and creating the gold standard of food safety systems. Putting that trust at risk in pursuit of a short-term political solution would be a serious mistake. Beef sold to American consumers should continue to meet rigorous, science-based food safety and inspection standards, regardless of the size of the processor. Advertisement “In just the past week, cattle producers have faced government intervention aimed at increasing foreign beef imports at prices below current levels. Now we are faced with another proposal aimed at changing how beef is processed. Constant government interference creates uncertainty for producers making long-term decisions about their businesses and the future of the cattle herd.” Trump signed a proclamation on Wednesday labeled “Further Ensuring Affordable Beef for the American Consumer.” The move, however, slated to begin Sept. 1, still doesn’t identify the source, provides 100,000 tons a month of imports and is only categorized as beef trim, that Trump encourages be sold at one-fourth below cost. The announcement sparked criticism from Congress and the industry. “The reality is this action will make it more difficult for American ranchers to rebuild our herd and bring prices down for the American people,” Sen. Tim Sheehy (R–Montana) said on his X account. “And most importantly, this will harm our ranching families who feed the nation.” But most pointedly, the criticism has come from cattle groups that do not want market interference at a critical point where producers are encouraged to rebuild their herds. Importing beef, some argued, triggers market price decline, and that penalizes producers just as they are working to determine whether to expand. “Cattle markets have already turned sharply lower this morning, to the detriment of farmers and ranchers. This is a critical time of year for cattle producers, as we approach the season where they are making decisions regarding their herds,” said NCBA CEO Colin Woodall on the same day as Trump’s post. “Cattle farmers and ranchers are responding to strong market signals and historically high demand, and we are already working to rebuild after years of ongoing drought, high input costs and other challenges that have reduced U.S. cattle numbers. Today's announcement and other market interventions throw cold water on the prospect of herd expansion and sacrifices long-term stability for short-term messaging.” The rallying cry went louder on Wednesday, as Woodall; Zippy Duvall, president of American Farm Bureau Federation; Jo Goggins, president of Livestock Marketing Association; Gene Copenhaver, NCBA president; and Justin Tupper, president of USCA signed a joint letter to Trump calling his plan “a disheartening message to farmers and ranchers across the country.” “American cattle producers are asking you for a fair and competitive market, honest price signals and policies that put U.S. farmers, ranchers and consumers first. We urge you to reverse course on this 90-day import plan and work with us instead on solutions that strengthen, rather than weaken, America’s capacity to feed itself,” the letter reads. Various state associations also weighed in after Trump announced his deal. The Kansas Livestock Association encouraged its members to reach their congressional delegation “and urge them to oppose plans to increase foreign imports.” The Colorado Livestock Association and Colorado Cattlemen’s Association also released a joint press release calling on Trump to reconsider his plan and “engage directly with cattle producers before advancing policies that could further disrupt livestock markets, and exercise greater caution when promoting policies that can have immediate effects on livestock markets.” As for the announcement Trump made to let farmers and ranchers process their own beef, the Meat Institute issued a statement Friday, criticizing the move as “the wrong approach” and a risk that undermines U.S. beef’s reputation for safety. “We are waiting to see more details from the administration; however, it is critical that expanding processing opportunities does not come at the expense of food safety,” the Meat Institute said in a press statement. “Federal and state inspection requirements exist for a reason: to help ensure that meat sold to American families is safe, wholesome and properly labeled. Allowing uninspected or inadequately inspected meat into the marketplace would put consumers at risk and undermine the food safety system that protects millions of Americans every day. “If the goal is to lower beef prices for American families, lowering food safety standards is the wrong response: America needs more cattle. Expanding the nation’s herd and increasing the beef supply will lower beef prices, preserve consumers’ and trading partners’ trust in the safety of the meat supply and strengthen the entire beef supply chain, from producers to consumers.
