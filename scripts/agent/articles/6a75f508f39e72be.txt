Home &raquo; SPONSORED BY Prorec From Food Industry Residues to Herd Performance: The Circular Feed Approach Behind Prorec September 1, 2026 SPONSORED As Quebec dairy producers look for ways to strengthen herd performance while reducing the environmental footprint of their operations, more are turning to food industry co-products – nutritionally valuable materials recovered from dairy processing, bakeries, biscuit makers and confectioners, which Prorec transforms into ready-to-use feed ingredients for livestock. Related Mineral nutrition is instrumental to herd performance Using feed efficiency and IOFC to monitor herd performance Midwest/North: Prepare for the cold to lift herd performance For 30 years, Prorec has specialized in this kind of valorization. The Quebec-based company recovers rejected and byproduct materials from the agri-food industry and turns them into ingredients that ruminants and other livestock can use safely and effectively. According to Stéphane Le Moine, co-founder and CEO of Prorec, the company's mission is straightforward: give renewed value to materials that still hold real nutritional benefit for farm animals, rather than letting them go to waste. A Simple Fit for Dairy Operations One of Prorec's flagship products is whey permeate , a liquid co-product of dairy processing that contains lactose, minerals and a high proportion of water. When incorporated into a total mixed ration according to a nutritionist's recommendations, whey permeate can supply part of the energy and mineral content a herd needs, sourced from a material that would otherwise go unused or misused. What makes this option attractive to many dairy farmers is how straightforward it is to implement. In most cases, all that’s required is a storage tank and a pumping system to receive and blend the product into the ration. Prorec doesn't just deliver the product and walk away – its team works directly with producers on needs assessment, nutritional analysis, supply logistics and ongoing technical follow-up. "For dairy co-products, we work mainly with dairy farms of more than 100 cows, but we can also serve operations of different sizes through various delivery options," notes Le Moine. That flexibility means the solution isn't reserved for the largest operations alone – midsized farms can access the same benefits through adapted delivery formats. Advertisement Prosucre: A Fast-Acting Energy Source Also With an Environmental Edge Alongside its dairy co-products, Prorec also offers Prosucre , an energy ingredient derived from the valorization of sugary food co-products, particularly well suited to ruminants and swine. Composed mainly of nonfibrous carbohydrates, Prosucre provides a source of energy that's rapidly available to the animal. In dairy cows, the simple sugars in Prosucre ferment quickly – and almost completely – in the rumen. That characteristic helps increase the energy density of the ration and stimulates rumen microbial activity, a key factor in nutrient utilization and overall herd performance. Its smell and taste also make it highly palatable, which typically makes it easier to integrate into existing feeding programs. Beyond its nutritional profile, Prosucre stands out environmentally. Based on external analyses using a net energy basis, its carbon footprint is roughly 51 times lower than that of corn grain used in animal feed. That difference comes down to sourcing: Prosucre is made from food co-products that already exist, rather than from crops grown specifically for feed. Research conducted by Agrinova has also observed an increase in milk protein percentage when Prosucre was incorporated into certain dairy cow rations. While results can vary depending on the herd and ration formulation, these findings point to real potential for Prosucre as a complementary tool to optimize feed performance while making use of circular-economy resources. Prorec's lineup doesn't stop there — the company also offers Pro-Biscuit , an ingredient derived from valorized bakery and biscuit-making residues, giving producers another option to diversify their feed sources. A Practical Fit for the Ration – and the Water Budget Beyond their nutritional and environmental profile, liquid dairy co-products like whey permeate offer a practical bonus: because they arrive in liquid form, they contribute to the water content of the ration. Depending on the product used, each litre delivered contains between 70% and 85% water – a meaningful advantage for operations facing limitations in water availability. Every farm has its own goals and constraints, and Prorec is clear that integrating these products should always be done in collaboration with the farm's feed adviser or nutritionist to make sure the ration stays properly balanced for herd health and performance. Making the Most of Resources Already in the System Beyond cost savings, using food co-products fits squarely within a circular economy approach , where materials from food processing are put back to their highest nutritional use once they meet regulatory requirements and animal needs – rather than being discarded. Prorec's own analysis shows that replacing a portion of conventional ingredients with valorized co-products can meaningfully reduce the carbon footprint associated with animal feeding. In 2024, the company estimates its activities helped avoid more than 45,000 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent , through both the valorization of food co-products and the displacement of conventional raw materials. Today, Prorec serves farms across Quebec. For a growing number of owners, Prorec's products represent an opportunity to diversify their supply sources, support herd performance and take part in better valorization of resources already being produced by Quebec's agri-food industry – all while reducing their carbon footprint. Ready to Explore Your Options? Every farm's ration, budget and constraints are different. If you're curious whether whey permeate, Prosucre or Pro-Biscuit could fit into your feeding program, the first step is a conversation with your nutritionist and the Prorec team.
