Advertisement Advertisement Advertisement Home &raquo; Blogs &raquo; Progressive Cattle From the Editor &raquo; The inconvenient reality of AI use The inconvenient reality of AI use David Cooper August 24, 2026 When technology’s progress moves at the speed of light, sometimes you feel like you’ve missed a few sunsets. Life just moves too fast, and our perspective can be changed in mere hours or even minutes. David Cooper Managing Editor / Progressive Cattle Email David Cooper That’s precisely the case with the rapid acceleration of AI (artificial intelligence) in the marketplace. When the popular AI large language models (LLMs) launched a few years ago, there was the predictable amounts of uncertainty and curiosity in the cultural landscape. How will it be used? Who creates this information? Will it be accurate and reliable? Will my kids now just cheat on their homework? We’ve moved beyond that point and very quickly. AI was adopted seamlessly into multiple industries. Wall Street ran in first and rewarded companies that promoted its capabilities. Software companies, hospitals, airports, internet and media all jumped on board to boast of the efficiencies created. Cities and counties jumped in line to host data centers. A tax symposium I attended included a speaker saying states were foolish not to recruit AI data centers. But that all changed quickly. The greedy got greedier. Wall Street got so high on AI speculation that it’s now perceived to be a bubble. We slashed the government and multiple agencies on the assumption that AI can replace people, but it can’t. The demand for data centers of excessive size and resource consumption was soon matched by a backlash that has turned into a wall of resistance. Advertisement On that last one, you can be sympathetic to communities concerned about how a data center will affect electricity rates, water consumption, climate impact and land usage. But now I believe some concerns have once again pushed the pendulum back too far. Recently, I was curious to see readers criticize the use of AI images in our publication, saying it leads to data centers destroying rural America. Here I must put myself in an uncomfortable bargain to defend something like AI I don’t necessarily care for. But defend it I will, with careful logic. AI usage in our magazine – as described in our Publisher’s Statement on the contents page – doesn’t replace our human abilities or contributions. But it can and should be harnessed and used wisely. If something cannot be illustrated by photos, user-generated design or illustration, we will use an AI program. Our understanding and research of issues can be enhanced by AI, if the sources are reliable. Using some basic philosophy, I'd contend that responsible use of AI must pass through three sieves, as Socrates once argued. Use it sparingly. Use it transparently. Use it ethically. Sparingly because AI shouldn’t be heavily relied on when our human talent remains viable and trusted. Use it too much and you lose credibility and relatability. AI is only utilized in uses where it was efficient and enabling for us. Transparently because responsible use of AI means telling our audience when we use it and when we don’t. We can write, illustrate and photograph using the senses and insight God and our schooling gave us. Ethically because the use of AI requires us to be responsible, with considerations that we do not falsify or categorically misrepresent something in our industry that is wrong, illegal or fraudulent. I stand behind those reasons for our using AI. If you have a problem with AI beyond that, your issue may be bigger than this magazine. AI platforms are in your banking apps, your gasoline pumps, your Instagram/Facebook/X accounts. They are used in healthcare, transportation, even your friendly weather report. The goals are usually for efficiency and productivity – not witchcraft or deception. The future and its tools can spread fear and falsehood. But properly applied, they give us the right pace of making today and tomorrow a better life.
