Holding them in our arms as infants, helping them through school, cheering on their events – these memories flood your brain when your kid gets married. When our children were little, someone told me, “The days are long and the years are short.” It’s true. When they’re little, it takes an immense amount of energy to cross the finish line at the end of the day, but somehow birthdays, Christmases and graduations zoom past us. Then suddenly, they’re at the altar. The wedding day was lovely. Chairs were put in a field next to an event barn. Tables were decorated inside. Food was prepped. Family began to file in, some who flew across the country to be there. Much like the rest of the summer, the day was hot. Since the West has been on fire, smoke has permeated many valleys across Montana, hiding the mountains. Yet that afternoon, a slight breeze cleared the smoke enough to see the outline of the mountains. Advertisement In the middle of the ceremony, a tractor started up in the distance as the neighbor of the venue began baling his hay. And at that moment, our little crew grew. The entire afternoon felt like life. Our life. We moved from one thing to the next, being fully alive, celebrating family expansion. Up until this time, expansion has often been in the context of ranching. From an industry standpoint, we discuss growth in terms of acres gained, herd numbers increased or diversification of income streams. We love discussing those aspects of ranching in our own operations and in others’. Yet, the growth that I most cherish is that of our family. Watching our son marry his high school sweetheart was full of joy because we gained another daughter, and her family gained another son. Our hearts are growing; our love is increasing. It will continue, too. One of our daughters became engaged this summer and will get married next summer. As parents, we are excited to join families and share life with more people. I had a different outlook when I married my cowboy. When we got married, we were focused on each other – and that’s the way it is meant to be. We thought of our future together and where we would go. Of course, we considered our families, but the perspective is different when you’re the one walking down the aisle. Our wedding took place at a little country church that my family attended when I was in high school. In true Montana fashion, it was right next to a small feedlot. The morning we got married, the feedlot crew decided they’d clean the pens. They were still cleaning them shortly before the ceremony. If you’ve ever been near a feedlot when they’re getting cleaned, you know how our wedding smelled. Many of our guests had not experienced that before, but they know now. Afterward, my cousin tied herbicide cans onto our pickup and wrote “Just Married” in the back window. Our little country wedding may have been a slight foreshadowing of our lives in ranching. The path hasn’t been as cut and dried as we anticipated, but I don’t suppose it ever is that way. I think our goals change over time, and we continue to move toward whatever goal is in front of us, marking milestones along the way. One of our kids getting married was a milestone. There will be others. One of the best pieces of advice given to me regarding milestones, particularly in reference to our son’s wedding, was, “Expect something to go not as planned.” That’s not an invitation to look for the bad or expect a shoe to drop, so to speak. It’s a partnership with reality that not everything turns out like you think it will. For our son’s wedding, we had a few small surprises, but nothing terrible. The dog room at the venue ended up being locked, and we couldn’t put a dog in there – but he quietly sat at his owner’s feet. We had to round up extension cords for the sound equipment. Someone stepped in as DJ when the original one had a family emergency. None of it distracted us from the moment – which was the second piece of advice I was given: “Be present, and enjoy it.” We took that advice to heart and truly had a wonderful time. We danced, we laughed and although it was a lot of work, we enjoyed it all. Memories of our kids being little flooded our brains, but I’m grateful to be present here in their adult years.
