Trump's plan to import beef harms both cattle, dairy industries In a move to combat high consumer beef prices, President Donald Trump announced a plan to allow more beef to enter the U.S. market. The plan allows up to 300,000 metric tons of beef – equivalent to more than 660 million pounds of beef – to be imported into the U.S. over a 90-day period without activating an “out of quota” tariff, which is a tax that goes into effect once a certain quantity of that product enters the country. Four of the nation’s leading livestock and agricultural organizations joined together to urge Trump to reverse the proposal. The American Farm Bureau Federation (AFBF), Livestock Marketing Association (LMA), National Cattlemen’s Beef Association (NCBA) and United States Cattlemen’s Association (USCA) sent a letter, calling on the Trump administration to work with industry stakeholders on solutions that keep food affordable while protecting producers, consumers and strengthening the nation’s ability to produce its own food. Advertisement “We share your goal of keeping groceries affordable for American families. However, flooding the market with discounted foreign beef is not the way to rebuild the American cattle herd, strengthen food security or lower grocery bills in a lasting way. This announcement has already driven cattle markets sharply lower and undermines producers at a critical time of year when they are marketing cattle and making herd-building decisions,” the letter stated. It continued, “Beef prices are in line with other consumer prices and reflect the inflated cost of raising cattle, grain and forage. Ranchers and farmers are finally experiencing the strong beef demand needed to invest in their operations after years of drought, high input costs, processing plant disruptions and other challenges that have reduced cattle numbers. This announcement will discourage investment in the U.S. cow herd and undo the progress producers have made. “We are deeply concerned that this import strategy sacrifices long-term food security for a short-term solution.” National Milk Producers Federation President and CEO Gregg Doud released a statement on the plan’s effect on the dairy industry. “Cull cow and calf sales are a key economic driver for U.S. dairy farmers and equates to 20 percent of annual dairy farm income, and greater than 20 percent of the U.S. beef production is now being supplied by dairy farms,” Doud said. “Policy-created disruption threatens the billions of dollars invested by U.S. dairy farmers and manufacturers to grow supply of beef and dairy products,” he added. America’s dairy industry delivers on second school nutrition commitment As students across the country return to the classroom, the International Dairy Foods Association (IDFA) announced that America’s dairy processors have fulfilled the Healthy Dairy in Schools Commitment, eliminating the use of certified artificial colors in milk, cheese and yogurt products sold to K-12 schools for the National School Lunch and School Breakfast Programs. The achievement marks the second consecutive year the U.S. dairy industry has delivered on a major voluntary commitment to improve school meals. Last year, processors met the Healthy School Milk Commitment, limiting added sugars in flavored milk to no more than 10 grams per 8-ounce serving – part of a broader trend that has cut added sugars in flavored school milk by 57% since 2006. This year, they removed certified artificial colors from school dairy products ahead of the 2026-27 school year. The dairy industry has also lowered added sugars in flavored yogurts to meet new school program standards, while preserving the taste and appeal that students choose to consume. Research shows removing flavored milk from school meals alone leads to a 37% decline in overall milk consumption. Together, the two commitments show an industry proactively investing, innovating and responding to evolving expectations while preserving student choice. fairlife celebrates progress on new facility in New York Last week, fairlife held a ribbon-cutting ceremony with New York Governor Kathy Hochul and local officials to mark the soft opening of the company’s new $650 million milk processing plant in Webster, New York. The 745,000-square-foot plant is fairlife’s flagship northeast facility, creating approximately 380 full-time local jobs and sourcing milk daily from more than 40 local dairy farms. It is expected to be fully operational later this year. Fluid milk sales up in June With nearly 3.3 billion pounds of packaged fluid milk products shipped by milk processors in June 2026, fluid milk sales were up 3.3% from June 2025. According to data from the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service:
Total sales: June 2026 sales of packaged fluid milk products were estimated at 3.3 billion pounds, up 3.3% from the same month a year earlier. At 21.1 billion pounds, year-to-date sales of all fluid products are 0.1% higher than last year.
Conventional products: Monthly sales totaled 3.1 billion pounds, up 3.3% from the same month a year earlier. Sales of flavored whole milk were 31.8% higher than last year. Year-to-date 2026 sales were estimated at 19.6 billion pounds, which is up 0.1% from last year.
Organic products: June sales totaled 239 million pounds, up 3.5% than a year earlier. Sales were up 9.4% for whole milk. Year-to-date organic fluid milk sales were estimated at 1.5 billion pounds, down 0.2% from this time last year. Organic represented about 7.2% of total fluid product sales in June.
The U.S. figures are based on consumption of fluid milk products in FMMO areas, which account for approximately 92% of total U.S. fluid milk sales, and adding the other 8% from outside FMMO-regulated areas. Sales outlets include food stores, convenience stores, warehouse stores/wholesale clubs, nonfood stores, schools, the food service industry and home delivery. NMPF: Milk production climbs on protein demand U.S. milk production grew 3% in June on a liquid basis as producers added 52,000 head to the milking herd to satisfy demand for protein – both milk and beef, according to a report from Katriel Marks-Yant at NMPF. Summarizing dairy markets in the August 2026 Dairy Management Inc./NMPF Dairy Market Report , Marks-Yant said, “Dairy protein demand has continued to support nonfat dry milk (NFDM) prices at the CME on account of limited supplies. Meanwhile, rising butter production continues to limit price gains, even as demand grows at home and abroad.” Domestic demand for cheese remains limited, but record export volumes are helping support cheese, and thus Class III, prices from sliding further. DMC margins were $10.88 per hundredweight (cwt) in June as feed prices and the All-Milk Price both slid. Expectations for the formula to reflect the recent increase in feed prices and a lower All-Milk Price set the stage for a potential DMC margin below the maximum payment threshold in August. For more information on commercial use, dairy trade, milk production, product inventories, prices and margins, view the August 2026 Dairy Market Report . AgCareers opens new U.S. scholarship opportunity AgCareers announced the return of its annual Feed Your Future Scholarship program, reaffirming its commitment to supporting the next generation of agriculture professionals. For 2026, the program has been expanded to provide scholarship opportunities to both a U.S. college student pursuing an agriculture-related degree and a high school senior planning to do so. Applications are now being accepted through Nov. 20. Applicants will share how the scholarship would support their future, what inspired their interest in agriculture and how they would introduce the value of agriculture to those outside the industry. For full details and to apply, visit the AgCareers Feed Your Future Scholarship website .
