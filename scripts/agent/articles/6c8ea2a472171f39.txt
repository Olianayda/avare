Farm bill extension expired on Sept. 30 As a new farm bill awaits consideration by the U.S. Senate, the latest one-year extension of the 2018 Farm Bill expired on Sept. 30, which will pause the Dairy Forward Pricing Program and the Dairy Indemnity Payment Program. The Dairy Margin Coverage (DMC) Program is unaffected since it was funded through 2031 as part of the One Big Beautiful Bill that was signed into law in 2025. The Continuing Appropriations, Agriculture, Legislative Branch, Military Construction and Veterans Affairs and Extensions Act, 2026, extended authorization of the Dairy Forward Pricing Program contained in the 2008 Farm Bill through Sept. 30, 2026. According to the USDA Agricultural Marketing Service (AMS) Dairy Program, this means that without continued authorization for the USDA to carry out this program, proprietary handlers establishing new forward contracts on or after Oct. 1, 2026, will not be exempt from paying minimum federal order prices. However, contracts established on or before Sept. 30, 2026, that extend through Sept. 30, 2029, are not impacted. If Congress fails to pass a new farm bill or issue an extension by Dec. 31, dairy policy would revert to “permanent law” under 1938 and 1949 statutes, which would force the USDA to support milk prices at parity-based levels near $50 per hundredweight, causing major market disruption. FSA interest rates up slightly in October The announced interest rates on loans through the USDA’s Farm Service Agency (FSA) are up slightly this month. As we begin October 2026, interest rates for operating and ownership loans (compared to September) are as follows: Advertisement
Farm operating loans (direct): 5.375%, up from 5.25%
Farm ownership loans (direct): 6.25%, up from 6%
Farm ownership loans (direct, joint financing): 4.25%, up from 4%
Farm ownership loans (down payment): 2.25%, up from 2%
Emergency loan (amount of actual loss): 3.75%, unchanged
The FSA also offers guaranteed loans through commercial lenders at rates set by those lenders. For more information, producers can contact their local USDA Service Center . NMPF: Rising feed and fuel costs tighten producer margin Liquid milk production grew 1.7% in July. While the size of the milking herd grew 1.9%, heat stress and wildfire smoke contributed to a slight decrease in milk output per cow, according to a report from Katriel Marks-Yant at National Milk Producers Federation (NMPF). Summarizing dairy markets in the September 2026 Dairy Management Inc./NMPF Dairy Market Report , Marks-Yant said, “Dairy ingredients are showing signs of strength. Nonfat dry milk prices rose into the 2.10s [dollars] in late September as domestic use rose and supply remained limited. Dry whey prices are elevated too as more of the whey stream heads into whey protein concentrates. “Conversely, even as butter is experiencing healthy domestic demand, growth in production volumes has outpaced domestic use. Cheese prices are also under pressure, feeling the effects of wavering consumer confidence as quick-service restaurant foot traffic falls and retail sales ease. Still, exports continue to see double-digit growth, helping maintain some market balance,” Marks-Yant added. Signs of increased economic pressure on producers are growing – feed costs are expected to rise through the end of the year, elevated diesel prices are increasing hauling charges, and borrowing costs are likely to rise due to the increase in bond markets and interest rates. For more information on commercial use, dairy trade, milk production, product inventories, prices and margins, view the September 2026 Dairy Market Report . Schreiber Foods announces $267 million investment in Missouri In late September, Schreiber Foods announced it will invest more than $267 million to expand its processed cheese network by building a new manufacturing facility in Carthage, Missouri. The investment will help Schreiber meet growing customer demand while strengthening its ability to deliver the exceptional quality, food safety and service customers depend on. The facility is expected to open in 2028 and create approximately 100 new jobs. For more than 75 years, Schreiber has been a vital part of the Carthage community, currently employing more than 1,300 people across its existing facilities. This investment reflects the company’s continued commitment to southwest Missouri. Alltech One Conference registration now open Registration is now open for the Alltech One Conference, which brings together global thought leaders and changemakers for an immersive exploration of science, sustainability and storytelling, and of the bold ideas shaping the future of agriculture. The conference regularly attracts an international audience, with past participants representing more than 90 countries. Building on more than 40 years of bringing together the agri-food industry, the conference returns home to Lexington, Kentucky, on May 24-26. In addition to its main stage program, the Alltech One Conference will feature 11 immersive content tracks covering aquaculture, beef, crop science, dairy, equine, pet, pork, poultry, food systems, business and talent, and Africa. Through expert-led sessions, dynamic panel discussions and real-world case studies, attendees will explore the innovations, technologies and ideas that are transforming the future of agriculture and food production. Across the conference program, attendees will learn about the trends shaping the future, from artificial intelligence and emerging technologies to sustainability, food security, global market dynamics and workforce transformation. Sessions will highlight innovative approaches to improving productivity, advancing animal and crop performance, strengthening business resilience and meeting the growing demand for nutritious, sustainable food around the world. Beyond the conference sessions, the Alltech One Conference will offer attendees a vibrant event experience designed to foster connection, conversation and community. Attendees can enjoy evening gatherings throughout the conference, including a music and food festival at Lexington Brewing & Distilling Co. Following the conference, there will be expanded opportunities to explore the region through specialized postconference tours. Early-bird registration is available for a limited time. Those who register now can access the lowest conference rates and be among the first to experience the keynotes, immersive tracks, networking opportunities and transformative discussions that define the Alltech One Conference. 2027 AABP Recent Graduate Conference registration is open The 10th Annual American Association Bovine Practitioners (AABP) Recent Graduate Conference, targeted for those AABP-member veterinarians who have graduated between 2019 and 2026, as well as any AABP-member credentialed veterinary technicians of any grad year, will take place Feb. 12-13 in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Registration for this in-person-only conference is now open online (you must be an AABP member and log in to register). This year’s conference theme is “Passion to Purpose.” “The early years in practice are pivotal for new graduates as they navigate the day-to-day challenges that come with rural practice as well as exploring disciplines within veterinary medicine that excite them,” explains 2027 Recent Graduate Conference Program Chair Dr. Monika Dziuba. “This conference provides the perfect mix of both, as it melds the traditional topics on clinical skills while introducing and expanding on various specialties within bovine medicine. We want to encourage new graduates to investigate new passions to supplement and energize them as they start their careers.”  The conference will offer topics covering beef, dairy, mixed animal, clinical skills and practice tips. Attendees will hear about the career and paths of experts sharing their knowledge and showcasing the diversified paths recent graduates may take on their own career trajectory. Back by popular demand will be preconference seminars on lameness, obstetrics, personal finance and using on-farm data for problem solving. This year’s conference offers 15.5 hours of continuing education (CE) in beef, dairy, clinical skills, practice tips and mixed animal topics (see the schedule and preconference descriptions ). Conference attendance is limited to current AABP members. Applications open for Alan Koepke Memorial Scholarships Calling high school seniors and college students with a passion for agriculture and the dairy industry: Applications for the Alan Koepke Memorial Scholarship are open through Dec. 1. The scholarship was established in 2025 to honor the life, legacy and values of Alan Koepke, whose commitment to agriculture, education and people left a lasting impact on the dairy industry. The scholarship fund was established through memorial contributions from family, friends and colleagues into Dairy’s Foundation, reflecting a shared belief in encouraging students who are committed to agriculture. Beginning for the 2027-28 educational year, the Alan Koepke Memorial Scholarship is expected to award at least one $2,000 scholarship to a high school student (planning to enroll in undergraduate education in fall 2027) and at least one $2,000 scholarship to a college student. The scholarship is open to students pursuing undergraduate education at an eligible Wisconsin institution, including:
University of Wisconsin Farm and Industry Short Course
Accredited technical schools or colleges within Wisconsin
Wisconsin universities with fields of study that may include agricultural business, animal science, food science or related areas
Applicants should demonstrate a genuine passion for dairy and a desire to use their education, career or talents to develop and support dairy farm owners and businesses that support the dairy industry. Scholarship recipients will be selected and notified in February 2027 and announced at the Professional Dairy Producers (PDP) Business Conference March 16-17. Application form and all details are available on the Dairy's Foundation website .
