Cows are the first thing most people picture when they think about a dairy farm’s methane emissions. But a sizable amount comes from manure storage, a source that farms have potential to influence. Liquid-manure methane is estimated to account for 20% to 30% of milk's carbon footprint, while manure management contributes roughly one-fifth of a dairy farm’s greenhouse gas emissions. As the Canadian dairy sector works toward its shared goal of net-zero emissions, manure storage is receiving renewed attention. Some of the most promising research is investigating a familiar tool: gypsum (calcium sulphate). Vicki Brisson Strategic Communications Manager / Dairy Farmers of Canada Email Vicki Brisson
