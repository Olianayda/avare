APHIS approves release of New World screwworm-infested zones in Texas The USDA’s Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) has approved the release of two New World screwworm (NWS)-infested zones in Texas, following concurrence from Texas’s state veterinarian, Dr. Bud Dinges, and the Texas Animal Health Commission (TAHC). This marks a significant milestone: the first NWS-infested zones to be released in Texas since the first case was detected in Texas on June 3. The two zones being released are:
Infested Zone 02.A (including portions of La Salle and Webb counties)
Infested Zone 03 (including portions of Gillespie, Kerr, and Kimble counties)
This action comes after state and federal subject matter experts confirmed that the two areas met all requirements outlined in APHIS’ New World Screwworm Response Playbook for zone release. Releasing these zones removes NWS‑related movement requirements for livestock and pets within the released areas, allowing producers to resume normal intrastate and interstate movement without NWS inspection. All non-NWS-related interstate movement requirements and international export requirements will still apply. Advertisement Several infested zones in Texas remain in place, and APHIS continues to work closely with TAHC to evaluate conditions for future zone releases. All Texas animal owners should visit the TAHC website to ensure they have the latest information about the location of infested zones, movement requirements and zone‑specific requirements.
U.S. and Taiwan dairy industries advance cooperation on dairy nutrition programs The U.S. Dairy Export Council (USDEC), the California Marketing Advisory Board (CMAB) and the Dairy Association of Taiwan (DAT) announced continued collaboration to promote the nutritional value of milk and milk products among key health officials and health practitioners as well the role of dairy in child nutrition. The announcement followed meetings in Taiwan between USDEC and CMAB leadership and Taiwanese government officials and dairy industry representatives. USDEC President and CEO Krysta Harden, joined by CMAB President and CEO Bob Carroll as well as executive vice president for policy development and strategy, Jaime Castaneda, and senior director of trade policy, Tony Rice, met with officials and industry leaders in Taiwan to discuss opportunities to strengthen dairy’s role in school meal programs and support broader nutrition education efforts across the market. The three organizations affirmed their shared commitment to expanding access to dairy for Taiwanese consumers and especially children through a joint effort to organize a seminar for nutritionists in Taiwan to further this goal. The initiative builds on the memorandum of understanding signed by USDEC and DAT in September 2025, as well as the U.S.-Taiwan Agreement on Reciprocal Trade finalized in February 2026, both of which laid the groundwork for deeper cooperation between the U.S. and Taiwanese dairy sectors. USDEC, CMAB and DAT will continue to coordinate details on the planned nutrition seminar in the coming months, with additional information to be announced as plans develop.
Dairy checkoff seeks entries for 2027 New Product Competition Dairy Management Inc. (DMI) is accepting entries for its 2027 New Product Competition, challenging undergraduate and graduate students across the U.S. to develop innovative dairy-based products for consumers seeking sustained energy. The annual competition gives tomorrow’s product developers an opportunity to apply their knowledge and creativity to develop dairy products that respond to current consumer and marketplace needs. The 2027 competition focuses on a changing consumer definition of energy. Rather than seeking a quick energy boost, consumers increasingly are looking for consistent, steady energy to support performance throughout the day. They are seeking products that can help them maintain energy, reduce fatigue, improve stamina and support better sleep. Successful entries will demonstrate innovation, consumer value and the effective use of dairy ingredients. Products must contain more than 51% dairy ingredients by weight and provide at least a good source of dairy protein. The competition is open to undergraduate and graduate students enrolled at U.S. colleges and universities during the 2026-27 academic year, including students graduating in May or August 2027. Students may enter individually or as teams under the supervision of a faculty adviser. The deadline for 2027 contest submissions is Feb. 1. A total of $27,000 in cash prizes will be awarded:
Platinum Dairy Innovator Award – $10,000 for first place
Gold Dairy Innovator Award – $7,000 for second place
Silver Dairy Innovator Award – $4,000 for third place
Dairy Innovator Awards – $2,000 each for three additional finalist teams
Six teams will advance to Phase 2, where they will submit their products for evaluation and present their concepts to a panel of dairy industry and DMI experts. The top three teams will be invited to attend and be recognized at the American Dairy Science Association Annual Meeting, June 27-30 in Raleigh, North Carolina. To learn more about the 2027 consumer and marketplace opportunity, view 2027 Challenge: Consumer and Marketplace Insights . Questions about the competition can be directed to Dr. Rohit Kapoor , vice president of product research at DMI.
Expo en Español offers practical insights for dairy professionals Expo en Español is World Dairy Expo’s educational program created specifically for Spanish-speaking professionals in the dairy industry. Presented entirely in Spanish, these seminars provide practical information and valuable tools for dairy owners, managers and mid-level managers. Expo en Español sessions are held Tuesday through Friday during World Dairy Expo in Mendota 1 of the Exhibition Hall at 10:30 a.m. Each session concludes with an opportunity for attendees to ask questions and engage with the presenters.
Tuesday, Sept. 29 Five key strategies to improve your dairy farm profitability Presenter: Paulina Letelier, consultant, GPS Dairy Consulting Letelier will be sharing five practical strategies that dairy managers and herdsmen can implement to improve farm profitability. Topics will include ways to increase milk production, improve feed intake, identify unprofitable cows and make better culling decisions to strengthen herd performance. The presentation is designed to provide practical, real-world examples that can be applied immediately on dairy farms.
Wednesday, Sept. 30 Prevention is better than cure – don’t leave mastitis to chance Presenter: Carolina Pinzón, dairy outreach specialist, University of Wisconsin – Madison, Division of Extension Reducing the risk of mastitis in dairy herds is key to long-term farm success. This presentation provides a practical framework focused on proven, research-based strategies across key areas, including people, environment, milking routine, mastitis management, milking equipment and herd health. Each area highlights simple practices to reduce bacterial exposure, support immune function and maintain udder health. The goal is to help dairy teams stay aligned, consistent and focused on what works to prevent mastitis.
Thursday, Oct. 1 Making future cows better with today’s data Presenter: João Dürr, CEO, Council on Dairy Cattle Breeding The data that leads to better cows is collected every day on dairy farms across the country. Learn how information used for day-to-day management decisions – like illness reporting, hoof trimming and animal ID – is applied to genetic evaluations and mating programs during this presentation. Recommendations for standardized data recording and how to include farm data in the National Cooperator Database will also be shared.
Friday, Oct. 2 The diarrhea dilemma: Practical tips to prevent and treat calf scours Presenter: Dr. Kendra Wells, veterinarian, Valley Veterinary Clinic This presentation will cover the key management factors associated with calfhood diarrhea, including diagnosis, early intervention, supportive care and appropriate antibiotic use. Practical strategies beginning in the maternity pen and extending through calf housing and feeding management will be discussed. Participants will also receive a refresher on team training, recognizing sick calves and implementing effective treatment protocols. Foremost Farms USA announces closure of Marshfield facility, teamsters in Reedsburg authorize strike Foremost Farms USA announced plans to close its Marshfield, Wisconsin, cheese manufacturing facility in December 2026 and discontinue production of 40-pound block cheese as part of a strategic realignment of its product portfolio to meet evolving market demands. Production is expected to continue throughout the transition period, with operations scheduled to conclude in December 2026. The decision follows a comprehensive review of changing dairy market dynamics, including increased U.S. cheese production capacity and shifting consumer demand. As Foremost Farms continues to evaluate opportunities to create long-term value for its members, customers and the cooperative, the company is focusing resources on areas that best position the business for future growth and success. The Marshfield facility, built in the 1950s, has a long history of producing award-winning cheeses. The planned closure will impact 68 employees who were informed of the decision on Aug. 24. Foremost Farms is committed to supporting affected employees throughout the transition by providing access to resources, training, human resources support and opportunities to apply for positions at other Foremost Farms facilities. At the Foremost Farms butter plant in Reedsburg, Wisconsin, members of Teamsters Local 120 voted overwhelmingly to authorize a strike. The group of 40 workers is fighting for a fair contract, stating the company fails to address chronic staffing problems, lagging wages and retirement security, and seeks concessions that would weaken existing overtime and attendance protections.
