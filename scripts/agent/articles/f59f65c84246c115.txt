Based on financial data in U.S. dollars, the combined sales of the world's 20 largest dairy companies were slightly up 5.4% from the year before. That followed 2024’s increase of 0.6% (U.S. dollar basis). Read: Rabobank: Subtle shifts for 2025, but a shake-up expected for 2026 Top 20 rankings Among the top 20 spots, European companies maintained their hold at 11, with companies in the U.S., China and Canada holding two spots each ( Table 1 ). Companies in New Zealand, India and Mexico rounded out the list. This year’s ranking saw notable movement, with five companies holding their spots from last year while DMK and Unilever exited following a merger and a spin-off, respectively, making way for new entrants Magnum and Emmi. “The global dairy industry is entering a new phase defined by consolidation, specialization and a greater focus on value creation,” said Lucas Fuess, senior dairy analyst at RaboResearch. “Companies increasingly view scale as a prerequisite for long-term competitiveness.” Advertisement Lactalis retained its position as the world’s largest dairy company for the fifth consecutive year, widening its lead over second-place Nestlé through a series of acquisitions. The top three companies in the ranking remained unchanged. Nestlé retained second place despite reducing dairy’s relative importance within its broader food and beverage portfolio. Third-ranked Dairy Farmers of America expanded through targeted investments in value-added processing, particularly specialty cheese. Rankings impacted by M&A, scale and specialization After decades in which growth was often driven by increasing milk volumes and geographic expansion, leading dairy companies are now prioritizing scale, profitability and strategic focus. At the same time, several companies are moving toward more specialized business models. One of the biggest changes this year came from the merger of Arla Foods (4) and DMK Group, which propelled the combined business ahead of Danone (5), despite its sales growth. China’s dairy giants Yili (No. 6) and Mengniu (No. 9) remain among the world's largest dairy processors, but both are navigating a more mature domestic market. As growth opportunities from volume expansion become harder to find, the companies are increasingly focused on premiumization, innovation, operational efficiency and higher-value products. Fonterra (10) dropped in the rankings after divesting its Mainland Group consumer business in Australia and Asia to Lactalis (1), allowing the cooperative to sharpen its focus on higher-return ingredients and food service channels. Unilever’s exit from the dairy rankings marks the end of an era. After separating its ice cream division into The Magnum Ice Cream Company, it no longer qualifies as a dairy company for ranking purposes. Magnum (11), meanwhile, entered the rankings as a standalone business built around some of the world’s most recognizable ice cream brands. FrieslandCampina (7), Savencia (13, Müller (14), Sodiaal (16) and Froneri (17) continue to represent an important tier of European dairy companies, but each is pursuing a different strategy as the industry undergoes consolidation. With the Arla-DMK merger, a spot opened in the top 20 for Emmi (19), Switzerland’s second-largest dairy company moved into the ranking, overtaking Grupo Lala (20) in the process.
