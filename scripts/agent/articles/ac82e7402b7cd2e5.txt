Checkoff Watch: More than a conference: Strengthening dairy’s place in schools September 16, 2026 Every school day, millions of children make an important decision: Will they drink the milk served in their cafeterias? Related Health & Wellness Watch: Fuel Up to Play 60 helps maintain dairy’s place in schools Checkoff Watch: U.S. dairy more than ready to share our story of sustainability Checkoff Watch: More kids, more schools and more milk: Fuel Up to Play 60 at 10 Behind that moment are thousands of school nutrition professionals who decide what foods are served, how menus are developed and which products make it into cafeterias. Their decisions influence the eating habits of millions of students every day, making them important partners in creating opportunities for dairy. This is why the dairy checkoff invests in building strong relationships with these decision-makers, including through our partnership with the School Nutrition Association (SNA). The farmer-founded National Dairy Council (NDC) attended this year's SNA Annual National Conference, where more than 6,600 school nutrition professionals, school nutrition directors and industry partners gathered in North Carolina. These are the people responsible for serving school meals across the country, and they are looking for practical solutions that help them provide nutritious, appealing meals while meeting evolving nutrition standards. Advertisement The conference is the checkoff’s opportunity to demonstrate dairy’s value, provide science-based nutrition expertise and strengthen relationships with the professionals who make decisions about how dairy is offered on students’ trays. Just as important, it gives school nutrition professionals the opportunity to meet dairy farmers and connect the milk they serve with the families who produce it. One of the strongest messages we heard was that school nutrition professionals value dairy and the support they receive from the checkoff. Through NDC and our network of state and regional dairy councils, the checkoff provides research, nutrition expertise, culinary resources and practical tools that help schools successfully include milk, cheese and yogurt on their menus. These resources help schools navigate changing nutrition standards while continuing to serve foods students enjoy. The conference also reinforced trends that create opportunities for dairy. School nutrition professionals are looking for practical menu solutions, and there is growing enthusiasm for dairy’s role in delivering high-quality protein and other essential nutrients. Recent changes surrounding whole milk in schools also generated considerable excitement, reinforcing dairy’s important role in supporting children’s growth and development. Representatives for the National Dairy Council promote dairy product consumption at the School Nutrition Association Annual National Conference in North Carolina. Image courtesy of Dairy Management Inc.  One of the most valuable aspects of the conference was making sure farmers were part of those conversations. Audrey Donahoe of New York and Arlene Vander Eyk of California spent time talking with attendees about how they care for their animals, protect natural resources and produce nutritious milk. Those conversations helped school nutrition professionals better understand the journey milk takes from the farm to the cafeteria, and the commitment farm families make every day. North Carolina farmer Roddy Purser also played an important role during a preconference workshop that combined a virtual dairy farm tour with a hands-on culinary experience. Participants learned firsthand about modern farming practices before preparing dairy-rich breakfast recipes designed to help schools meet nutrition standards. The feedback spoke for itself. Participants shared that they gained a greater appreciation for farmers and a better understanding of the care, technology and regulations involved in producing milk. These experiences help build confidence in dairy while creating meaningful connections between farmers and the professionals serving students every day. Beyond those interactions, the checkoff hosted culinary demonstrations, showcased dairy-forward recipes through our S mart Swaps initiative and provided nutrition education. School nutrition professionals left with practical tools they can use, from student-tested recipes to menu planning resources and culinary training designed specifically for schools. Our NDC booth also created opportunities for hundreds of one-on-one conversations. Attendees sampled milk, cheese and yogurt, explored dairy nutrition resources and learned more about how dairy can help schools deliver nutritious, student-approved meals. By the end of the conference, nearly 700 school nutrition professionals had requested follow-up information from NDC, creating relationships we hope will continue long after this event. When farmers and importers invest in the checkoff, they are investing in relationships like this. Every conversation with a school nutrition director, every educational session and every opportunity for a farmer to tell their story helps strengthen dairy’s position in schools. Those relationships help shape how the next generation experiences dairy and ensure milk remains a trusted, nutritious choice in cafeterias for years to come. Working with SNA is another important way the checkoff engages with the people making decisions about school meals, creating opportunities for more children to enjoy the wholesome foods produced by America’s dairy farm families. This column was written by Katie Bambacht, vice president of nutrition affairs for National Dairy Council.
