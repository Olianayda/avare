Home &raquo; Progressive Dairy &raquo; Early-life management focus of PDP Calf Care Connection Dairy Industry News Early-life management focus of PDP Calf Care Connection August 24, 2026 One-day workshops set for Oct. 14, 15 in Wausau, Fennimore. A lifetime of good health and productivity starts on a dairy calf’s first day. The 2026 Calf Care Connection workshop presented by Professional Dairy Producers (PDP) will focus on the latest research and real-world experiences that can turn early-life management into the foundation of a productive and profitable dairy herd. Related Manure management focus of Waste to Worth Conference PDP calf facility tours to feature three Wisconsin dairy farms Calf Care Workshops to be held Oct. 19 & 20 in Wisconsin Two repeating sessions of the one-day workshop will be held on Wednesday, Oct. 14, at North Central Tech College in Wausau, Wisconsin, and on Thursday, Oct. 15, at Southwest Tech College in Fennimore, Wisconsin . Each workshop begins with registration at 9 a.m., with sessions running from 9:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. Simultaneous Spanish translation will be offered for both sessions of the program. “We know the decisions we make from the time a calf is born will impact her health and production for the rest of her life,” said Laura Raatz, Oconto Falls dairy producer and calf care manager and PDP board director. “Everyone on your calf care team will benefit from learning together with experts and peers at PDP’s Calf Care Connection.” A combination of hands-on, interactive breakout sessions and panel discussions will provide research-backed information along with insights from experienced calf managers. Calf care team members, calf managers, veterinarians and dairy calf specialists will all benefit from sessions focused on how starter grain intake shapes early rumen and gut development, managing milk as a foundation for calf growth, and how calf housing shapes health, behavior and growth outcomes. PDP Calf Care Connection is accredited for up to 4.75 Dairy AdvanCE (DACE) and up to four ARPAS continuing education credits.
