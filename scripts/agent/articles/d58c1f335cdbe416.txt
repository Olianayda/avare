Future Breeder Program to build next generation of dairy breeders Patryce Eldredge LeFevre September 11, 2026 GenoSource to introduce the Future Breeder Program at this year’s World Dairy Expo by awarding five youth contestant winners with embryo packages. While youth spend countless hours preparing their cattle for the show ring, they don't always have control over the genetics behind the animals they lead into the ring. Their success in showmanship and fitting competitions is driven by skill, dedication and hard work, yet the opportunity to own elite genetics isn't always within reach. Patryce Eldredge LeFevre Editorial Intern / Progressive Dairy Related The importance of financial literacy for the next generation of dairy farmers Engaging in Excellence: Hand in hand with the next generation of dairy Foundation Watch: Donations help recruit the next generation of dairy leaders A new program debuting at World Dairy Expo this year aims to change that by rewarding youth who demonstrate a passion for the dairy industry with elite embryo packages. The Future Breeder Program by GenoSource will give youth the opportunity to raise high-quality genetics of their own and take their enthusiasm for dairy breeding to the next level. “Showmanship and fitting are the areas you have a bit of control over. If you put in a lot of work and effort into honing the skills these competitions are asking for, you're going to hopefully do very well. The one area we know dairy youth maybe don't have as much control over is the animals they get to take to the show ring,” says Laura Demmer, director of marketing at GenoSource. “We [GenoSource] are focused on genetics, both elite-type and high-performance commercial animals, and if we can help youth get an animal that takes their passion and success to a new level, that's the whole purpose of what we're doing.” Demmer shares that this program is an opportunity to encourage dairy youth to become dairy breeders. How the program will work Five embryo packages will be awarded at World Dairy Expo, each containing three embryos. Advertisement Three embryo packages will be awarded through the showmanship contest, with one package going to a recipient in each age division: junior, intermediate and senior. Winners will be randomly selected from the top 10 finalists of those divisions. Two additional packages will be awarded through the fitting competition, one each in the intermediate and senior divisions. The winners will be randomly selected from the top five individuals from both the males and females in the fitting contest. Helping youth hone their skills Demmer says it is critical to support the next generation of the dairy industry, and programs such as this one support the talent, passion and spark youth have. Through this program, youth are provided with experience and skills they can take into their future careers. The award of embryos through the program leans on GenoSource’s expertise and niche market in embryos but also gives youth a hands-on opportunity to further hone their skills that are important to helping the dairy industry prosper. “We believe it’s something that will set them up for success,” Demmer says. “It's going to teach them communication skills, organization skills, and they're going to have to learn about the technology that goes into this as well as marketing skills on how they get this information out and get people interested in their cattle.” After being awarded an embryo package, the selected youth will need to coordinate the logistics of their embryos and then work with their family or another farm to set up recipients for the embryos. Additionally, youth can learn more about science and genetics through genomic testing on their resulting calves. Demmer says this program is designed to give back to the youth in a hands-on way that encourages them to develop skills they may not have had before. “If we know that those individuals are putting in the effort to be in that top 10, and really going for it, maybe we can help them have an animal that they are excited to take in the ring,” Demmer says. “We try to find as many avenues as we can to support youth who are going to give back to the industry.
