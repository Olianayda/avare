Silage can be created from nearly any forage crop, including grasses and legumes, but corn is often used because it is the most energy‑dense. Putting up a crop as silage preserves forage quality through fermentation. It must have sufficient moisture and be stored anaerobically (oxygen‑free) under conditions that encourage sugars to ferment into organic acids. The acidity generated by these acids (mainly lactic, with some acetic and propionic) and the lack of oxygen prevent development of spoilage‑causing microbes.
