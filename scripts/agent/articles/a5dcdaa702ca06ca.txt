Madison, October 2025. Aaron Eaton takes one look and says it out loud: "when that 5-year-old came in the ring today, it was game over." Lovhill Sidekick Kandy Cane — Grand Champion of the International Holstein Show, Reserve Supreme of World Dairy Expo.
Krohlow had her purple twice before Eaton ever laid a hand on her. He staked his reputation on that cow in two separate rings while the rest of the industry was still deciding. Now he holds the microphone at the show that made her famous — and Kandy Cane, since scored EX-97 , has not been seen in a ring all year.
Neither has the Junior Champion who stood beside her in that final callout. Neither has the Reserve Junior Champion, the Honorable Mention Intermediate Champion, or the Honorable Mention Senior Champion. Of the nine animals who took a divisional banner in Madison last October, five have not surfaced at any of the sixteen major North American Holstein shows held in 2026 — a majority of that callout has been dark for eleven months.
One of the others surfaced in April, in Chilliwack, and won.
A caution before you bet anything: World Dairy Expo does not publish a public Holstein entry list, and entries closed August 31. Nobody outside these barns knows who is on the truck. What follows is who has earned a place in the conversation — and who has been conspicuously, deliberately quiet.

Two Grand Champions. One class. A rematch of the closest call of last year's show. That's Friday's headline, and we'll get to why it's a fight between two completely different kinds of cow.
The Show You're Walking Into
The scale, from last year: 468 Holsteins — 236 heifers, 169 intermediate cows, 63 mature cows. The winter heifer calf class alone ran 61 head , the spring calves 42. Judge Aaron Eaton spent two days on it.
The week starts earlier than most people think. The  International Junior Holstein Show goes Friday, October 2nd at 10:00 a.m. , official Ben Wallace of Stoughton, Wisconsin with associate Phillip Topp of Botkins, Ohio. That's the junior-exhibitor ring, so it only catches the animals owned by juniors — but that list is not short this year, and it includes some of the best cattle in this preview. One eligibility caveat, because it decides which day you watch. Several animals in this preview carry Red or red-carrier names — Cherry Lor Robin-P-Red, Golden Oaks I Altitu-Red-ET, Hilrose Ac Attababy-Red-ET, Carpsdale-J Fall-Red-ET, Ms Be Tempted-Red-ET among them. Registered Red & Whites show in the International Red & White Show , heifers Wednesday, September 30 at 2:00 p.m. and cows Thursday, October 1 at 7:00 a.m. — a different ring on a different day. Which ring any individual animal lands in depends on her registration, and we can't confirm entries. Don't be surprised to find one or two of these names in Wednesday's lineup instead of Thursday's.
Watch five and you've watched the show: Glitter Girl in the winter yearlings, Abudahbi in the junior threes, the three-way four-year-old fight, Aria in the five-year-olds, and the aged cows, where two 2026 Grand Champions and the cow who won this ring last October could end up in the same lineup.
What Krohlow Rewards, In His Own Words
He's a fitter first — 18 years at it, plus three as a professional hoof trimmer, running HammerTime on 40 acres at Poynette, Wisconsin with his wife Haley and four kids, Callie, Conway, Cooper and Case, off eight to ten show heifers a year. By his own official Expo biography he prepared 17 Grand Champions at World Dairy Expo and nine at the Royal during that fitting career, in Canada, England, Germany, Italy, Sweden, Switzerland and Mexico.
His stated ideal: "cows that are square frame, cows that are balanced and hard top, cows that walk out extremely comfortable front and rear," with "tremendous udders, quality, texture, and symmetry." In heifers, animals "extremely comfortable in their locomotion" with "quality from end to end" that "track straight both front and rear feet."
Two tiebreakers repeat often enough to bet on. He splits close pairs on rib — "a tad more drop of rib," "spring and openness all the way to her barrel," "more depth right up in that fore rib." And he reads the back of a rear udder hard enough to separate third from fourth on "more definition to see on the back of that rear udder" and "more seam, all the way at the bottom of that udder."
He also forgives youth, praising one winter two-year-old as "a February heifer just moving 20 days out of being a junior two-year-old" with a "tremendous future" — and putting her top of the class over cows with more milk behind them.
The Fitter's Eye: Why Over-Bagging Will Backfire
Eighteen years of fitting means eighteen years of hands on udders the night before a show. A man with that history knows the difference between glandular texture and fluid sitting in the rear seam, and he knows exactly what a cow looks like when she has been held too long — because at some point in 18 years he has been the one asked to do it.
His own reasons tell you he reads for the real thing rather than the volume. He moves cows up on "veination and quality all the way through her mammary system," on "tightness right at the top of the rear udder attachment," on "more quality and texture" in a fore udder, on "definition of the seam" at the bottom of the rear. Every one of those is a texture word, not a capacity word. And three years of commercial hoof trimming sit underneath the rest of it: he splits close cows on "bone and flatness right down in the hock," on pasterns, on cows that "track so comfortably, so straight." Extra weight in an udder shows up first in exactly the places a trimmer looks — a cow who roaches over her loin to carry it, or who stops handling her hocks cleanly by the second lap.
The rules are on his side, and they are not vague. World Dairy Expo lists "improper fitting practices, such as over filling and over bagging" as an ethics violation outright, alongside "treating an animal, particularly the udder, without a verifiable veterinary need… for the purpose of improving the appearance" and using any device to artificially create or enhance the udder crease. Overfilling udders carries a one-year suspension as a Category 1 violation; udder alterations are Category 2, a minimum two-year ban — and a second Category 1 inside three years is elevated automatically.
Enforcement is not theoretical either. The top one-third of every class is ultrasounded , and champions are ultrasounded and milk-sampled after their champion class and again after Supreme. Udder ultrasounds, milk sampling, hair pulls and blood draws are four of the ten testing actions every exhibitor consents to at entry. Expo also endorses the PDCA Show Ring Code of Ethics, adopted February 2025.
So the read for Friday: pushing extra capacity into a four-year-old or an aged cow to force the look of bloom risks two separate problems. It risks a judge who has spent two decades learning what fluid looks like under the lights and who scores on texture and seam definition rather than volume — and if her rear teats come off their plumb line or her top goes soft carrying it, he sees it before she finishes half a lap. Then it risks the ultrasound afterwards.
The Associate Factor: Kelly Barbee in the Big Heifer Classes
The associate is Kelly Barbee of Concord, North Carolina, and he is there because Krohlow put him there — at World Dairy Expo the official judge submits three names for associate, and the selection is then approved by a committee of Expo exhibitors. This is a pairing the official wanted.
Barbee is a territory sales manager for Trans Ova who has exhibited over 30 All-Americans or All-American nominations across multiple breeds , previously developed and boarded high-end show cattle for clients in the U.S. and Canada, and managed a large commercial herd for ten years before that. His two biggest assignments are both colored-breed: the 2010 World Dairy Expo Jersey Show and the 2008 Royal Winter Fair Jersey Show .
That Jersey pedigree is the thing to watch Thursday afternoon. A judge who has made his name sorting Jerseys is a judge trained on dairyness, clean thurls, open rib and silky hide rather than sheer scale — and Thursday's first three classes are where those eyes earn their money. Last year the winter calf class ran 61 head and the spring calves 42. In a class that size nobody evaluates 61 animals equally; the depth of the lineup gets sorted while the top end is being walked. A coarse, round-boned calf with extra throat flesh can hide in the middle of a 60-head class under some panels. Against this one, expect her parked early.
Thursday: The Heifers
Spring Calf — the one class with no history
Born March through May of this year, so nobody holds a form line — which means pedigree is most of what you have to go on.
Do-N-Joy Major Jax-ET won 22 head at Midwest Fall for Brooklyn, Bryce & Blakely Kohls, Arlington, MN, with HM Junior Champion of the Junior Show. She's a Robella Major out of Cresentmead Jaurora-Red-ET , born March 26. Major is the frame-and-cleanliness side of this cross; the red-carrier dam line is where the dairyness comes from, and it's worth knowing which one she took after when she walks in — Major daughters that inherit too much substance from the maternal side get coarse, and this judging panel will not reward that.
WSC TStorm Rattlin Bog-ET (Thunder Storm) topped 38 at Eastern Fall for Hayden Weaver, Ephrata, PA — biggest spring calf class of the fall circuit. B-Will Mas Dancing Diva-ET (Master) won the class in the Coliseum itself at Midwest Summer National for Charlotte Wingert and Grace Salter, Pearl City, IL. Also Cornerest Altitud Magpie-ET (Altitude), 28 head at Northeast Fall for Kialah Nelson, and Winright Major Erica , 40 head at Ontario Summer, bred and owned by Brian Enright.
Two more with provincial credentials: Petitclerc Airdrop Samantha won the spring heifers at Le Suprême Laitier, bred and owned by Ferme Jean-Paul Petitclerc & Fils of St. Basile, QC, and Cobequid Tatoo Calypso (Tatoo) won the spring calves and took HM Junior Champion of the Atlantic Summer Classic for Ferme Val D'Espoir at Saint-Philippe-de-Neri, QC. In a class this young, a provincial final win in September is worth as much as a bigger class in August.
Winter Calf — the Canadian nobody has faced
Blacklilly Direct Lala-ET (Direct) won the winter calves at Northeast Spring for Eaton Holsteins, Glamourview Farms and Zeh & Cates Morrill of Marietta, NY — out of 12 head, which is the honest caveat on her, because two heifers in this class beat fields three and four times that size.
Extondale Master Mae (Master) won 45 head at Ontario Summer for Beckridge Holsteins, Keswick, ON — the largest heifer class held anywhere in Canada this year — and has never crossed the border. Against her: Genosource Mjr Heartatak-ET (Major) out of 40 at Eastern Fall for Billy Jo Rhodes and Chris & Jennifer Hill, Utecht Major Superstar (Major), Junior Champion of the Midwest Fall Junior Show bred and owned by Kolton Utecht, Fairmont Brielle-ET (Lombardi), Reserve Junior Champion of the Northeast Fall Junior Show for Ella Purchase, and Heritage Crazy Drama (Delta-Lambda), class winner at Midwest Summer National for Madison & Melanie Sarbacker.
